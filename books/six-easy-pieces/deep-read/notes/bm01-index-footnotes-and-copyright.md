## Leading propositions

- Footnote 1, to ch01's remark that carbon may be graphite or diamond: a diamond can be burned in air (p. 100).
- Footnote 2, to ch02's table of interactions: defines strength and the sign for of the order (p. 100).
- Footnote 3, to ch03's sentence that the stars are made of the same atoms as the earth: Feynman says he is rushing, that each sentence of the story could be a lecture, and then answers the poets who say science takes away from the beauty of the stars (pp. 100–101).
- In footnote 3: knowing what the stars are adds to what one sees and feels; nothing is mere; the truth is more marvellous than artists of the past imagined (pp. 100–101).
- Footnote 4, to ch04's derivation: the point is the possibility of reaching the result by theoretical reasoning, not the result, which the student may already know (p. 101).
- Footnote 5, to ch04's conservation of baryons: antibaryons count as minus one (p. 101).
- Footnote 7, to ch05's moon calculation: the fall is measured from the straight line tangent to the orbit at the point where the moon was a second earlier (p. 101).
- Footnote 8, to ch05's claim that the orbit can be proved to be an ellipse: the proof is not given in this course (p. 101).
