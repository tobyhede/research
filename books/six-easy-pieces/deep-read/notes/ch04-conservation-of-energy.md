## Question this chapter answers

What does the law of conservation of energy say, what kind of statement is it, and how can a formula for one form of energy be found by reasoning alone?

## Key terms

| Term | First used | Author's meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| energy | p. 57 | A numerical quantity that does not change through all the changes nature undergoes. Not a substance and not a mechanism; physics does not know what it is (p. 59). |
| conservation of energy | p. 57 | The law that this quantity stays the same, with no known exception. |
| perpetual motion | p. 59 | For weight-lifting machines: a net lifting of a weight after the machine has been returned to its original condition with no outside source. |
| reversible machine | p. 60 | An ideal machine that can run equally well backward; unattainable in practice. |
| gravitational potential energy | p. 62 | Weight times height, summed over the objects; the energy an object has because of its position relative to the earth. |
| potential energy | p. 62 | The general name for energy that depends on location relative to something else. |
| principle of virtual work | p. 64 | Using a small imagined motion of a structure that is not really moving to apply conservation of energy and find the forces in balance. |
| kinetic energy | p. 64 | Energy of motion: the weight times the height the object could rise to by virtue of its velocity. |
| heat energy | p. 65 | Not a new form: kinetic energy of the random internal motion of atoms. |
| elastic energy | p. 65 | The energy of a stretched spring; at bottom like chemical energy, the attraction of atoms for each other. |

## Leading propositions

**What is energy?**

- There is a law governing all natural phenomena known to date, with no known exception: a certain quantity, energy, does not change (p. 57).
- The law is abstract and mathematical; it describes no mechanism (p. 57).
- The analogy: a mother counts a child's 28 indestructible blocks and, as blocks become hidden, adds terms computed from the weight of a box and the height of bathwater, so that the total stays 28 (pp. 57–58).
- The feature to abstract from the story is that there are no blocks (p. 58).
- Two points carry over: energy can enter or leave the system and must be accounted for, and it has many forms, each with its own formula (pp. 58–59).
- Physics today has no knowledge of what energy is, and no picture of it as little blobs of definite amount (p. 59).

**Gravitational potential energy**

- Much about nature can be extracted from a few facts and close reasoning; the argument that follows is modelled on Carnot's on steam engines (p. 59).
- Hypothesis: there is no perpetual motion with weight-lifting machines (p. 59).
- No machine can lift a weight higher than a reversible machine can (p. 60).
- All reversible machines lift to exactly the same height, whatever their mechanism (pp. 60–61).
- For a machine that lowers one pound one foot and lifts three pounds, that height is a third of a foot; in general weight times height is unchanged (pp. 61–62).
- The reasoning might not be true, because nature does not have to follow it, so it must be checked; experiment confirms it (p. 62).
- In general the change in energy is the force times the distance through which it acts (p. 62).
- The many school rules for levers, pulleys and inclined planes are all this one principle (pp. 62–63).

**Kinetic energy and other forms**

- A pendulum at the bottom of its swing has lost height and kept the ability to climb again, so there is energy of motion (p. 64).
- The potential and kinetic formulas are both approximate, the first failing at great heights and the second at high speeds, but the law itself is exact once the exact formulas are used (pp. 64–65).
- Real machines stop; the energy has gone into the random motion of atoms, which a thermometer detects (pp. 65–66).
- Large-scale experiments cannot strictly demonstrate conservation of energy or build a reversible machine, for the same reason (p. 66).
- Chemical energy is partly kinetic energy of electrons and partly electrical energy (p. 66).
- Nuclear energy has formulas and no known fundamental law (p. 66).
- A positron and an electron at rest can disappear and release a definite radiant energy fixed by their mass (p. 66).
- We do not understand the conservation laws deeply (p. 66).
- In quantum mechanics conservation of energy is tied to the fact that things do not depend on the absolute time; momentum to independence of place; angular momentum to independence of orientation (p. 67).
- Three further laws are simple counts: charge, baryons and leptons (pp. 67–68).
- Conserved energy is not the same as available energy (p. 68).
- Controlled energy from hydrogen would remove the need to worry about supply; this is put as a task for physicists (p. 68).

## Arguments

- **Premises**: (p. 60) suppose a machine B lifts three units higher than reversible machine A does for the same drop of one unit + (p. 60) lower the load from B's height to A's, gaining free work, then run A backward to restore the one-unit weight.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 60) both machines are back at the start with work left over, which is perpetual motion. So B cannot lift higher than A.
- **Premises**: (p. 60) the same argument applies with A and B exchanged when B is also reversible.
  **Conclusion**: (pp. 60–61) all reversible machines lift the same height, so the height can be found without looking inside the machine.
- **Premises**: (p. 61) a reversible machine lowers one ball one foot and raises a rack of three balls by a distance X + (p. 61) with shelves spaced X apart, the net effect is to lift one ball a distance of three X + (p. 61) if three X exceeded a foot, or fell short of it when run backward, there would be perpetual motion.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 61) three X equals one foot. Generalised: the sum of weights times heights does not change (p. 62).
- **Premises**: (p. 63) on a three-four-five inclined plane, a balanced arrangement is reversible + (p. 63) one pound rises three feet while the hanging weight falls five.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 63) the hanging weight is three-fifths of a pound.
- **Premises**: (p. 65) springs and levers stop moving + (p. 65) they are then warmer by a definite amount.
  **Conclusion**: (pp. 65–66) the energy became kinetic energy of atoms; heat is not a separate form.
- **Premises**: (p. 67) assume an experiment behaves the same whenever it is done + (p. 67) add the principles of quantum mechanics.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 67) conservation of energy can be deduced. Feynman adds that whether the assumption is strictly true is not known.

## Evidence offered

- The screw jack: a twenty-inch handle and ten threads to the inch lift a ton with about 1.6 pounds of force (p. 63).
- The loaded bar: a 55-pound lift balances 60 pounds at the middle and 100 pounds a quarter of the way along (pp. 63–64).
- Stevinus's chain draped over a triangle, which does not go round by itself (p. 63).
- Thermometers showing that a stopped spring is warmer (p. 65).
- The positron and electron annihilating (p. 66).
- Only one part in two billion of the sun's output falls on the earth (p. 68).
- Footnote 4: the point of the derivation is the possibility of reaching the result by theoretical reasoning, not the result (p. 101). Footnote 5: antibaryons count as minus one baryon (p. 101).

## Open questions

- The derivation assumes no perpetual motion, and Feynman notes that this assumption is itself a general statement of the law being derived (p. 59). So the argument does not establish conservation; it shows what form the gravitational term must take if the law holds. He does not present it as more than that, and footnote 4 confirms the aim (p. 101).
- If energy is only a number computed by formulas, what distinguishes a law of nature from a bookkeeping convention that could always be rescued by adding a term? The block story invites the question (p. 58). Feynman's implicit answer is that each new term has its own formula that can be checked independently, but he does not state it.
- Entropy and the laws of thermodynamics are named and not explained (p. 68).
- The closing forecast about energy from hydrogen (p. 68) is a hope, not a finding.
- The missing equations mean the exact forms of 4.3 and 4.6 cannot be quoted from this text. The prose gives them as weight times height (p. 62) and as a function of velocity found from the height an object could reach (p. 64).
