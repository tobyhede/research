## 30-second brief

- **Problem:** science explains how the brain discriminates, integrates and reports. Why is any of that accompanied by experience?
- **Thesis:** experience cannot be explained in physical terms, because the physical facts do not entail it. Materialism is therefore false. Experience should be treated as a fundamental feature of the world, tied to physical processes by laws that a science of consciousness must discover.
- **Who benefits:** anyone who wants the strongest statement of the hard problem and of property dualism, including those who intend to reject both.
- **Worth reading?** Yes, selectively. Chapters 1, 3 and 7, with the first and last sections of chapter 4, carry most of the value. The author gives the same short tour himself.

## 3-minute mental model

### Core ideas, ranked

1. **Consciousness and awareness are different things.** Awareness is a functional notion: information available for the control of behaviour. Consciousness is what it is like. Most theories of consciousness explain awareness. [Ch 1, line 608](./source/source.txt#L608) [Ch 1, line 612](./source/source.txt#L612)
2. **Explaining something physically means showing that the physical facts entail it.** That works for life, learning and nearly everything else, because those notions are defined by structure and function. [Ch 2, line 794](./source/source.txt#L794) [Ch 2, line 1126](./source/source.txt#L1126)
3. **Experience is the exception.** A physical duplicate of me with no experience, a zombie, involves no contradiction. So the physical facts leave experience open and no physical explanation can close the gap. [Ch 3, line 1172](./source/source.txt#L1172) [Ch 3, line 1272](./source/source.txt#L1272)
4. **So materialism is false, and the replacement is naturalistic dualism.** Experience is a further fact about the world, governed by new fundamental laws that supplement physics and leave it intact. [Ch 4, line 1432](./source/source.txt#L1432) [Ch 4, line 1478](./source/source.txt#L1478)
5. **The price is that experience explains nothing we do, including what we say about it.** Chalmers calls this the paradox of phenomenal judgment and accepts it. [Ch 5, line 1940](./source/source.txt#L1940) [Ch 5, line 2002](./source/source.txt#L2002)
6. **Consciousness and awareness go together by law, with matching structure.** This is the bridge that lets neuroscience study consciousness at all. [Ch 6, line 2334](./source/source.txt#L2334) [Ch 6, line 2476](./source/source.txt#L2476)
7. **Experience depends on functional organization, not on what a system is made of.** Replace neurons with chips one by one and experience neither fades nor flips. So the right program, run on anything, would be conscious. [Ch 7, line 2532](./source/source.txt#L2532) [Ch 9, line 3144](./source/source.txt#L3144)
8. **Speculation: information has a physical and a phenomenal aspect.** Taken without restriction this gives experience to thermostats. The author rates it more likely wrong than right. [Ch 8, line 2888](./source/source.txt#L2888) [Ch 8, line 3114](./source/source.txt#L3114)

### Three things to remember

- The thesis is conditional and the author says so: if one takes consciousness seriously, his position is where one ends up. He admits he cannot prove the antecedent, and that past a point the two sides cannot argue across the divide. [Introduction, line 322](./source/source.txt#L322) [Introduction, line 318](./source/source.txt#L318) [Introduction, line 320](./source/source.txt#L320)
- Zombies are claimed to be logically possible, not possible in our world. In our world, by natural law, anything organized like a conscious brain is conscious. [Ch 3, line 1184](./source/source.txt#L1184) [Ch 7, line 2782](./source/source.txt#L2782)
- Confidence falls through the book. Chalmers would bet fairly confidently that experience is fundamental, weakly that it is everywhere, and is torn on the quantum-mechanical conclusion. [Ch 10, line 3566](./source/source.txt#L3566)

### What it changes in the reader's thinking

After this book it is hard to hear a theory of consciousness without asking which thing it explains: the functions (discrimination, report, attention, self-monitoring) or the fact that performing them feels like something. Chalmers gives a precise form to the suspicion that the second question survives any answer to the first, by tying explanation to entailment and showing where entailment stops. A reader who rejects his dualism still has to say which step fails: that zombies are conceivable, that conceivability shows possibility, or that materialism needs entailment. The book also separates two things that are often run together. One can hold that experience is not reducible and still hold, as Chalmers does, that brains and suitably organized machines have it by law.

## 10-minute deep brief

### 1. Two concepts of mind

- **Author's claim:** mental terms lead a double life. On the phenomenal concept a mental state is characterised by how it feels; on the psychological concept, by what it does. Consciousness in the sense that matters is the first. Awareness is the name for its functional counterpart. [Ch 1, line 458](./source/source.txt#L458) [Ch 1, line 608](./source/source.txt#L608)
- **Reasoning:** functional analysis works for learning, memory and belief, because those are defined by causal role. It fails for experience, because a state could play the role with no experience attached. The mind–body problem then splits into an easy part about psychology and a hard part about why psychology is accompanied by experience. [Ch 1, line 502](./source/source.txt#L502) [Ch 1, line 560](./source/source.txt#L560)
- **Evidence:** a catalogue of experiences, and a review of how Descartes folded the psychological into the phenomenal and later functionalists did the reverse. [Ch 1, line 376](./source/source.txt#L376) [Ch 1, line 450](./source/source.txt#L450)
- **Boundary:** the claim that role without experience is coherent is asserted here and argued in chapter 3. **[Analysis]** The two-way split is stipulated early, and it is what makes a zombie describable; someone who rejects the split has rejected the book's argument at page one.
- **Application [Application]:** when reading any theory of consciousness, ask whether it explains awareness or experience. Chalmers applies the test to global workspace, multiple drafts and neural-oscillation theories in chapter 3. [Ch 3, line 1316](./source/source.txt#L1316)

> “Consciousness is always accompanied by awareness, but awareness as I have described it need not be accompanied by consciousness.” [Ch 1, line 612](./source/source.txt#L612)

### 2. Supervenience and what explanation requires

- **Author's claim:** B-facts supervene on A-facts when fixing the A-facts fixes the B-facts. The dependence is logical when the A-facts entail the B-facts, and natural when it holds only by a law of nature. A phenomenon can be reductively explained exactly when it supervenes logically on lower-level facts. [Ch 2, line 660](./source/source.txt#L660) [Ch 2, line 674](./source/source.txt#L674) [Ch 2, line 794](./source/source.txt#L794)
- **Reasoning:** a reductive explanation analyses the concept, usually by function, then shows how lower-level processes perform the function. If entailment fails, a further question always remains: why is this process accompanied by the phenomenon? Materialism is the thesis that the physical facts entail every positive fact. [Ch 2, line 756](./source/source.txt#L756) [Ch 2, line 736](./source/source.txt#L736)
- **Evidence:** biology, chemistry, economics and ordinary objects all pass the test; even God could not make a world physically like ours and biologically different. The exceptions are consciousness, indexical facts, negative facts, and laws and causation. [Ch 2, line 682](./source/source.txt#L682) [Ch 2, line 1078](./source/source.txt#L1078) [Ch 2, line 1126](./source/source.txt#L1126)
- **Boundary:** the technical core is the two-dimensional account of meaning. Each concept has a primary intension (what it picks out if a given world is actual, knowable a priori) and a secondary one (fixed by how the actual world turned out). Water is necessarily H2O on the second and not on the first. Chalmers uses this to argue that conceivable worlds are possible worlds. [Ch 2, line 878](./source/source.txt#L878) [Ch 2, line 978](./source/source.txt#L978) He grants the definition of reductive explanation has an element of stipulation. [Ch 2, line 798](./source/source.txt#L798)
- **Application [Application]:** to test whether X is reducible to Y, ask whether a complete Y-description leaves X open as a matter of meaning, not of current knowledge.

> “a reductive explanation is a mystery-removing explanation.” [Ch 2, line 802](./source/source.txt#L802)

### 3. Why consciousness cannot be reductively explained

- **Author's claim:** consciousness does not logically supervene on the physical, so no explanation given wholly in physical terms can account for it. [Ch 3, line 1158](./source/source.txt#L1158)
- **Reasoning:** five arguments. A zombie twin is coherent. So is a twin with inverted colour experience. We know of consciousness only from our own case, and nothing in the physical story would lead us to posit it. Mary, who knows all the physical facts about colour vision from a black-and-white room, does not know what red is like. And no functional or structural analysis of the concept is available. [Ch 3, line 1168](./source/source.txt#L1168) [Ch 3, line 1208](./source/source.txt#L1208) [Ch 3, line 1224](./source/source.txt#L1224) [Ch 3, line 1238](./source/source.txt#L1238) [Ch 3, line 1250](./source/source.txt#L1250)
- **Evidence:** case studies of actual theories. Cognitive models, neurobiological accounts, appeals to quantum physics and evolutionary stories each explain a function or presuppose the link to experience. Physics gives structure and dynamics, and from those one gets only more of the same. [Ch 3, line 1316](./source/source.txt#L1316) [Ch 3, line 1348](./source/source.txt#L1348) [Ch 3, line 1384](./source/source.txt#L1384) [Ch 3, line 1404](./source/source.txt#L1404)
- **Boundary:** Chalmers answers the vitalism comparison by saying that life is defined functionally and experience is not. [Ch 3, line 1288](./source/source.txt#L1288) He concedes that a scenario can seem conceivable through a hidden a priori mistake, as with a false Fermat's last theorem. [Ch 3, line 1202](./source/source.txt#L1202) **[Analysis]** The five arguments share one premise, that there is something to explain beyond functions; he admits as much in replying to the charge of circularity. [Ch 3, line 1306](./source/source.txt#L1306)
- **Application [Application]:** a claimed explanation of consciousness should say where the entailment comes from. If it needs a bridging principle it has not explained experience; it has assumed a law.

> “Why should all this structure and function give rise to experience?” [Ch 3, line 1272](./source/source.txt#L1272)

> “The process of natural selection cannot distinguish between me and my zombie twin.” [Ch 3, line 1404](./source/source.txt#L1404)

### 4. Naturalistic dualism

- **Author's claim:** experience exists; a physically identical world without it is logically possible; so facts about experience are further facts; so materialism is false. What replaces it is a property dualism in which experience is fundamental and depends on the physical through psychophysical laws. [Ch 4, line 1432](./source/source.txt#L1432) [Ch 4, line 1470](./source/source.txt#L1470)
- **Reasoning:** the main objection is that zombies may be conceivable and still impossible, as water without H2O is. Chalmers replies that such cases only change how a world is described, and that the argument runs on primary intensions, where no such gap arises. A stronger, brute necessity would be an unexplained primitive. [Ch 4, line 1506](./source/source.txt#L1506) [Ch 4, line 1560](./source/source.txt#L1560)
- **Evidence:** the model is Maxwell: when electromagnetism could not be derived from existing physics, new fundamental properties and laws were added. [Ch 4, line 1474](./source/source.txt#L1474) He also sorts the alternatives into type A (deny the gap), type B (accept the gap, keep materialism) and type C (property dualism). [Ch 4, line 1818](./source/source.txt#L1818)
- **Boundary:** the physical world stays causally closed, so explanations of behaviour need not mention experience. Chalmers does not call this epiphenomenalism but accepts that a weak form follows. [Ch 4, line 1710](./source/source.txt#L1710) [Ch 4, line 1746](./source/source.txt#L1746) One way out: physics describes only relations, and experience may be the intrinsic nature of what it describes. [Ch 4, line 1694](./source/source.txt#L1694) Against type A he says the dispute ends in a clash of intuitions. [Ch 4, line 1836](./source/source.txt#L1836)
- **Application [Application]:** locate any position on consciousness by which of four claims it denies: experience exists, it is not entailed by the physical, materialism needs entailment, physics is causally closed. [Ch 4, line 1750](./source/source.txt#L1750)

> “it is a bridge that argument cannot cross.” [Ch 4, line 1836](./source/source.txt#L1836)

### 5. The paradox of phenomenal judgment

- **Author's claim:** judgments and claims about consciousness are behaviour and cognition, so they can be explained physically. Consciousness cannot. Therefore consciousness is explanatorily irrelevant to what we think and say about it. [Ch 5, line 1940](./source/source.txt#L1940)
- **Reasoning:** the zombie twin makes the same claims, writes the same book and is wrong. Chalmers keeps every premise and argues that the view survives: consciousness is the thing to be explained, not a posit brought in to explain behaviour, so explaining the reports does not explain it away. [Ch 5, line 1966](./source/source.txt#L1966) [Ch 5, line 2038](./source/source.txt#L2038)
- **Evidence:** an account of knowledge by acquaintance. Having an experience puts one in a direct epistemic relation to it; this, not a causal chain from experience to belief, is what justifies the belief. The zombie has the belief without the evidence. [Ch 5, line 2120](./source/source.txt#L2120) [Ch 5, line 2130](./source/source.txt#L2130)
- **Boundary:** he calls the objection the most powerful argument for the other side. [Ch 5, line 2036](./source/source.txt#L2036) **[Analysis]** The reply says what justifies the belief but not why the belief and the experience line up, other than by law. This is the book's most exposed point and the author does not hide it.
- **Application [Application]:** treat first-person reports as data about cognition that need explaining in their own right. Chapter 8 uses exactly this as a lead toward a theory. [Ch 8, line 2914](./source/source.txt#L2914)

> “The trick is to learn to live with the combination.” [Ch 5, line 2002](./source/source.txt#L2002)

### 6. Coherence between consciousness and cognition

- **Author's claim:** a theory of consciousness consists of psychophysical laws. The first: where there is consciousness there is awareness, meaning information directly available for the global control of behaviour, and the structure of experience mirrors the structure of what is represented in awareness. [Ch 6, line 2238](./source/source.txt#L2238) [Ch 6, line 2340](./source/source.txt#L2340) [Ch 6, line 2334](./source/source.txt#L2334)
- **Reasoning:** there is no outside test for experience, so theory-building relies on the first-person case, on the assumption that reports are mostly reliable, and on simplicity. The correlation in our own case is too systematic to be an accident. Any further physical ingredient required for experience would be an arbitrary extra. [Ch 6, line 2254](./source/source.txt#L2254) [Ch 6, line 2476](./source/source.txt#L2476) [Ch 6, line 2498](./source/source.txt#L2498)
- **Evidence:** colour space, the visual field, blindsight and related cases, where absence of experience goes with absence of the right kind of availability. [Ch 6, line 2316](./source/source.txt#L2316) [Ch 6, line 2352](./source/source.txt#L2352)
- **Boundary:** the laws are not fundamental and may not be strict. Empirical work cannot test them, because it presupposes them: evidence that a brain process goes with consciousness is always evidence that it goes with awareness. [Ch 6, line 2470](./source/source.txt#L2470) **[Analysis]** The notion of awareness is adjusted several times in the chapter to keep the match.
- **Application [Application]:** read claims about neural correlates of consciousness as claims about the neural basis of availability, plus this bridging assumption. [Ch 6, line 2440](./source/source.txt#L2440)

### 7. Organizational invariance

- **Author's claim:** any system with the same fine-grained functional organization as a conscious system has qualitatively identical experiences, whatever it is made of. [Ch 7, line 2532](./source/source.txt#L2532)
- **Reasoning:** two thought experiments. Replace my neurons with functionally identical silicon, one at a time. If the end product had no experience, then along the way there would be a subject, Joe, whose experience has faded while he insists nothing has changed. If the end product had different experience, one could install a switch and make experience jump back and forth with the subject unable to notice. Both results are far less plausible than invariance. [Ch 7, line 2580](./source/source.txt#L2580) [Ch 7, line 2612](./source/source.txt#L2612) [Ch 7, line 2702](./source/source.txt#L2702) [Ch 7, line 2734](./source/source.txt#L2734)
- **Evidence:** thought experiments only, which Chalmers calls plausibility arguments. [Ch 7, line 2554](./source/source.txt#L2554)
- **Boundary:** the result is about what the laws of our world allow. Absent and inverted qualia stay logically possible, so this is functionalism without reduction. [Ch 7, line 2782](./source/source.txt#L2782) **[Analysis]** Both arguments assume a subject's judgments are fixed by organization, which sits uneasily with chapter 5's claim that experience partly constitutes the content of beliefs about it.
- **Application [Application]:** chapter 9 turns this into a case for strong AI. A system implements a computation when its causal structure mirrors the computation's formal structure, and a simulation of a mind is a mind because being conscious depends only on organization. [Ch 9, line 3172](./source/source.txt#L3172) [Ch 9, line 3284](./source/source.txt#L3284)

> “The crucial feature here is that Joe is systematically wrong about everything that he is experiencing.” [Ch 7, line 2612](./source/source.txt#L2612)

> “These arguments from thought experiments are only plausibility arguments, as always, but I think they have considerable force.” [Ch 7, line 2554](./source/source.txt#L2554)

### 8. Information, panpsychism and quantum mechanics

- **Author's claim:** a fundamental law may connect experience to information, understood as a difference that makes a difference. Information has a physical aspect and a phenomenal aspect. [Ch 8, line 2874](./source/source.txt#L2874) [Ch 8, line 2888](./source/source.txt#L2888)
- **Reasoning:** the same structured space shows up in experience and in the brain states underlying it; information is both simple and independent of substrate; and what explains our reports about experience, access to bare locations in an information space, is a natural place to look for the basis of experience. [Ch 8, line 2900](./source/source.txt#L2900) [Ch 8, line 2914](./source/source.txt#L2914)
- **Evidence:** none beyond fit with the earlier principles. Unrestricted, the principle gives a thermostat three very simple experiences, and Chalmers argues there is no principled point at which experience should switch off. [Ch 8, line 2962](./source/source.txt#L2962) [Ch 8, line 2958](./source/source.txt#L2958)
- **Boundary:** he says this chapter is the most likely to be entirely wrong, that panpsychism is not the foundation of his view, and that the key to a theory probably lies elsewhere. [Ch 8, line 2806](./source/source.txt#L2806) [Ch 8, line 3006](./source/source.txt#L3006) [Ch 8, line 3114](./source/source.txt#L3114) The hardest problem is getting unified human experience out of micro-level experience. [Ch 8, line 3090](./source/source.txt#L3090)
- **Application [Application]:** chapter 10 applies the theory to quantum mechanics. Chalmers rejects the idea that consciousness collapses the wave function, argues that a superposed brain would carry several subjects each seeing a definite world, and so finds Everett's interpretation the most attractive, with its probabilities unexplained. [Ch 10, line 3404](./source/source.txt#L3404) [Ch 10, line 3490](./source/source.txt#L3490) [Ch 10, line 3550](./source/source.txt#L3550)

> “Experience is information from the inside; physics is information from the outside.” [Ch 8, line 3072](./source/source.txt#L3072)

## Critical assessment

Everything in this section is **[Analysis]** unless a locator attributes it to Chalmers.

- **Strongest contribution:** the separation of consciousness from awareness, and the demonstration, theory by theory, that explanations of the second are routinely offered as explanations of the first. This stands whether or not one accepts dualism.
- **Weakest or least-supported claim:** within the main argument, the step from conceivability to possibility. The zombie premise rests on an intuition of coherence and a rule about the burden of proof [Ch 3, line 1186](./source/source.txt#L1186); the exclusion of a stronger kind of necessity rests on there being no reason to believe in it and on what God could have created. [Ch 4, line 1566](./source/source.txt#L1566) Outside the main argument, the information principle of chapter 8 makes no specific predictions, as the author says.
- **Hidden assumptions:** that reductive explanation requires entailment a priori; that physics deals only in structure and dynamics; that psychophysical laws will be few, simple and non-arbitrary, like physical laws; that a subject's judgments are fixed by functional organization.
- **Meaningful counterargument:** the paradox of chapter 5, turned around. If everything Chalmers believes and writes about experience would be believed and written by a zombie, then his conviction that there is a further fact is fully explained without the fact. A type-A materialist says that this is the whole story. Chalmers's reply is that he has evidence the zombie lacks; the materialist replies that the zombie says the same. Chalmers accepts that argument cannot settle this and reports that audiences side with him by two or three to one. [Notes, line 3838](./source/source.txt#L3838)
- **Important question left unanswered:** what the fundamental laws are. The book ends with constraints on them and one admittedly unlikely candidate. Also open: why judgment and experience line up if neither explains the other; which physical information carries experience; how many subjects a brain contains.

## Action layer

This is a work of theory and prescribes little. The items below are **[Application]**, derived from tests the book states.

- **Try now:** take any account of consciousness you find persuasive and ask the chapter 3 question of it: once the mechanism is fully described, is it still an open question why it is accompanied by experience? [Ch 3, line 1272](./source/source.txt#L1272) Then place the account on the chapter 4 map by which premise it denies. [Ch 4, line 1750](./source/source.txt#L1750)
- **Conditions required:** the tests presuppose the distinction between awareness and consciousness. If you do not grant that there is a further phenomenon, the tests return nothing, and the author agrees. [Introduction, line 320](./source/source.txt#L320)
- **Warning signs:** a theory that defines consciousness as report, access or self-monitoring and then explains that; a neural correlate presented as an explanation; an appeal to quantum effects or complexity that explains more structure and dynamics. [Ch 3, line 1256](./source/source.txt#L1256) [Ch 3, line 1384](./source/source.txt#L1384)
- **Failure modes of the book's own approach:** the positive theory rests on plausibility judgments that cannot be tested from outside, and its bridging principles cannot be contradicted by data. [Ch 6, line 2470](./source/source.txt#L2470)
- **Seven-day experiment:** write one paragraph stating which of the four premises in chapter 4 you reject and why, then read the section of the book that answers that rejection and note whether your paragraph survives.

## Reading recommendation

**Read selected chapters.** Follow the author's short tour: chapter 1, the early parts of chapter 2, all of chapter 3, the first and last sections of chapter 4, the opening of chapter 6 and chapter 7.
