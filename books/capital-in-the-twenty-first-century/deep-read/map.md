- **Kind**: theoretical for three parts (what happened to the distribution of income and wealth since the eighteenth century, and why), practical in Part Four (what institutions should regulate capital).

## The whole-book question

Does the dynamics of private capital accumulation lead inevitably to ever greater concentration of wealth, as Marx believed, or do growth, competition and technology reduce inequality in later stages of development, as Kuznets thought, and what follows for the twenty-first century? (p. 3)

## Unity statement

Piketty assembles tax, estate and national-accounts series for more than twenty countries over three centuries to show that the capital/income ratio and wealth concentration followed a U-shape: high in the Belle Époque, collapsed by the shocks of 1914–1945, and recovering since the 1970s. He explains the pattern with β = s/g and α = r × β and argues that when the return on capital r exceeds growth g, as it did before 1914 and likely will again as growth slows, inherited wealth comes to dominate and inequality becomes arbitrary and incompatible with meritocratic democracy. His remedy is a progressive global tax on capital, backed by financial transparency and regional political integration, alongside a modernised social state and progressive income tax.

## High-value chapters for this reading

Ranked against the reading purpose: "Understand and critically evaluate Piketty's argument in full, and leave notes solid enough to support later cross-book thematic syntheses." Confirmed by the reader.

1. fm01 — states the thesis, the forces of convergence and divergence, and the method.
2. ch05 — the second fundamental law and the forecast that β rises as g falls; the core mechanism.
3. ch06 — whether r falls as β rises (elasticity of substitution above 1); the hinge on which the capital-share claim rests and the most contested chapter.
4. ch10 — the explanation of extreme wealth concentration by r > g and the role of the 1914–1945 shocks.
5. ch11 — inheritance and meritocracy; the normative core of the "arbitrary inequality" charge.
6. ch15 — the global capital tax, the main policy conclusion.
7. ch09 — supermanagers and institutional explanations of labor-income inequality; the US case that does not fit r > g.
8. ch17 — the compressed restatement of the whole argument.
