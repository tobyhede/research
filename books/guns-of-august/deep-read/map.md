## Classification

- **Kind**: theoretical in Adler's sense (it explains what happened and why); the author also intends a warning.
- **Genre**: narrative military and diplomatic history, written for general readers from published memoirs, diaries and official documents. Read with the history strategy: separate what she reports from what she is trying to prove, and ask whose testimony each scene rests on.

## The whole-book question

Why did the war of 1914 open as it did, and how did the plans, people and miscalculations of its first month turn an expected short war into a deadlock that no one could end?

## Unity statement

Four general staffs went to war with rigid plans built on assumptions about each other that were false, and on a shared belief that the war must be short. In August 1914 each plan was carried out against the evidence and each failed: the French offensive collapsed on the frontiers, the Russian one at Tannenberg, and the German sweep through Belgium, weakened by its author's successors and by the enemies and detachments it created, turned short of Paris and was caught on the Marne. Because no battle of the first month was decisive, and because German conduct in Belgium and the aims each side adopted closed off compromise, the war went on.

## High-value chapters for this reading

Ranked against the reading purpose: "what does Tuchman argue about why the war opened as it did and why its first month fixed its shape, and how good is her evidence?"

1. af01 — the only place the thesis is stated outright, with the chain of ifs.
2. ch02 — the Schlieffen plan and its assumptions; everything German follows from it.
3. ch03 — Plan 17 and the refusal of evidence; everything French follows from it.
4. ch06 — the plan overrides the sovereign on 1 August; the clearest single demonstration.
5. ch14 — the French plan fails; the German left is overcommitted.
6. ch21 and ch22 — why the German right turned, why it was caught, and who decided to fight.
7. ch17 — why the war could not be ended by negotiation; the best-evidenced chapter.
8. ch19 — the arithmetic of the weakened right wing; Paris and Gallieni.
9. ch16 with ch15 — Tannenberg and the corps drawn east.
10. ch13, ch09 — both headquarters facing contrary evidence; the short-war belief and the price of Belgium.

Lower for this purpose: ch01 (scene-setting), ch05 (portraiture), ch10 and ch18 (the sea; self-contained), ch12, ch20, fm01.
