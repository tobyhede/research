# ch15: "The Cossacks Are Coming!" (pp. 223–245)

## Question this chapter answers

How did Russia open its promised offensive into East Prussia between 12 and 24 August, and how did the German command there nearly collapse and then recover?

## Key terms

| Term | First used | Author's meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forward mobilization | p. 225 | Russia's improvised scheme of skipping preliminary stages to attack by the promised date, starting without its supply services. |
| Northwest Army Group | p. 225 | Jilinsky's command over the First and Second Armies; his method of coordination was to tell Samsonov to hurry. |
| First Army (Vilna) | pp. 227-228 | Rennenkampf's 200,000 men advancing through the Insterburg Gap to draw the Germans. |
| Second Army (Warsaw) | p. 228 | Samsonov's force coming round the lakes from the south through roadless sand, two days behind. |
| Six days | pp. 228-229 | The interval in which the Germans would face only one Russian army; known from the April war games and left uncorrected. |
| Wireless in clear | p. 229 | Russian orders sent uncoded for want of wire, codes and cryptographers. |
| Eighth Army | pp. 229-230 | The German force in East Prussia, about equal to either Russian army, under orders to defend the province without being overwhelmed. |
| Gumbinnen | pp. 233-235 | The battle of 20 August: François won on the left, Mackensen's corps broke in the centre; on the whole a Russian victory. |
| Retreat behind the Vistula | pp. 235-236 | Prittwitz's intention after Gumbinnen, which cost him his command. |
| Hoffmann's plan | pp. 237-238 | Move the whole Eighth Army by rail and march against Samsonov, leaving a screen before Rennenkampf. |
| Hindenburg and Ludendorff | pp. 238-241 | The pair appointed on 22 August: a proven staff officer and a calm retired general with a von. |

## Leading propositions

- [narrative] France pressed Russia daily to attack at once; Russian orders dropped the proviso about sufficient strength by 10 August (pp. 224-225).
- [interpretation] The gap between promise and preparation was plain to the Russian command from the start (pp. 224-225).
- [narrative] The Russian army had 418 motor transport vehicles; supply past the railhead went by horse (pp. 225-226).
- [narrative] Refugee tales from East Prussia began to distract the German Staff from the West as early as 4 August (pp. 226-227).
- [narrative] Rennenkampf invaded on 17 August; the different railway gauge meant the Russians could not use East Prussian lines (pp. 227-229).
- [interpretation] German artillery made two German divisions the equal of three Russian (p. 229).
- [narrative] François disobeyed Prittwitz and fought at Stalluponen on 17 August, taking 3,000 prisoners (pp. 231-232).
- [narrative] The Germans read Rennenkampf's halt order, sent in a simple code (p. 233).
- [narrative] At Gumbinnen on 20 August Russian guns ran out of shells on the right, while in the centre Russian heavy guns routed Mackensen's corps (pp. 233-235).
- [narrative] Rennenkampf did not pursue; his supply was failing and he could not use the railways (pp. 235-236).
- [interpretation] The likelier explanation of the halt is fatigue and supply, as Clausewitz described, and not treason (pp. 235-236).
- [narrative] Prittwitz telephoned OHL that he would retire to the Vistula and might not hold it; Moltke saw that reinforcements could only come from the Western Front (pp. 236-237).
- [narrative] Hoffmann persuaded the Staff to turn against Samsonov instead; no one told OHL the retreat was cancelled (pp. 237-238).
- [narrative] Moltke replaced Prittwitz with Hindenburg, with Ludendorff as Chief of Staff; Ludendorff's first orders matched Hoffmann's (pp. 238-239).
- [narrative] Jilinsky drove Samsonov's exhausted and unfed army forward while Rennenkampf moved west toward Königsberg, away from the junction (pp. 242-244).
- [narrative] On 24 August Samsonov's orders for the next day were intercepted in clear, and the Eighth Army committed everything against him (pp. 244-245).

## Arguments

- **Premises**: (pp. 224-225) French pressure + (p. 225) an attack date four days ahead of the supply services + (pp. 228-229) a known six-day gap between the two armies.
  **Conclusion**: (pp. 242-243) the offensive was launched in a form its own planners expected to fail.
- **Premises**: (p. 236) Rennenkampf's transport was in chaos + (p. 236) he could not use the German railways.
  **Conclusion**: (pp. 235-236) his halt needs no conspiracy to explain it. She argues this against a literature of accusation.
- **Premises**: (pp. 236-237) Prittwitz reported retreat and asked for reinforcements + (p. 238) his staff reversed the decision and did not report it.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 237) OHL formed its picture of a collapsing East on information already out of date. This is the root of the transfer of corps in ch16 (pp. 247-248).

## Evidence offered

- Paléologue's diary for the French pressure and the scene of the Cossack general (pp. 223-224).
- Russian General Staff orders of 6 and 10 August (p. 225).
- Gourko's account of the first raid (p. 227).
- Hoffmann throughout, for the German headquarters; she calls one of his reports malicious and distorted and notes that the record of the night of 20 August is not clear (pp. 235-236, 238).
- François's own account of his disobedience (pp. 231-232).
- Samsonov's telegrams to Jilinsky on the state of his men (p. 243).
- The British liaison officer with the Second Army on Samsonov (p. 242).
- Sourcing: I did not read this chapter's endnotes. The German side is Hoffmann, François, Ludendorff and Hindenburg, rivals for credit. The Russian side comes from émigré generals (Gourko, Golovin by implication), from Knox and from the French ambassador. The judgment on Rennenkampf is hers, supported by Clausewitz and not by a Russian document.

## Open questions

- If Gumbinnen was a Russian victory and East Prussia could be held by Hoffmann's plan, how real was the danger that frightened OHL?
- She treats Hoffmann as both the mind behind the victory and an unreliable narrator of it. Which of his claims are corroborated?
