# ch21: Von Kluck's Turn (pp. 333–347)

This note was written before ch19 and ch20 were read. Those chapters cover the retreat, the Battle of Guise and Gallieni's appointment; see their notes.

## Question this chapter answers

Why did Kluck turn inside Paris between 30 August and 3 September, what did each headquarters make of it, and how did the French come to see the opening?

## Key terms

| Term | First used | Author's meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inward wheel | pp. 333-334 | Kluck's change of direction from south to southeast, passing east of Paris to roll up the French left instead of enveloping the capital. |
| The correct solution | p. 336 | What German training taught a general to find: destroy the enemy's field armies first and leave a fortified camp until afterwards. |
| The gaps | p. 334 | Twenty-mile intervals between the armies of the German right wing, which reinforcements from the left were meant to fill. |
| In echelon | pp. 338-339 | Moltke's order of 2 September that Kluck follow behind the Second Army as flank guard; Kluck ignored it. |
| General Order No. 4 | p. 340 | Joffre's order of 1 September continuing the retreat toward the Seine and Aube, with no date for the counter-offensive. |
| Sixth Army | pp. 339, 341 | Maunoury's force, placed under Gallieni on 1 September as the mobile troops of the fortified camp of Paris. |
| Fortified camp of Paris | p. 341 | Gallieni's command; Joffre had it put under his own authority the next day so that he could use its troops in the field. |
| Fagalde's map | p. 343 | The blood-smeared map taken from a dead German officer on 1 September showing every corps of Kluck's army marching southeast. |
| Joffre-Gallieni controversy | pp. 340-341 | The later dispute over who originated the Battle of the Marne. |

## Leading propositions

- [narrative] On 30 August Kluck judged the British and French beaten and saw that the French line did not extend as far west as expected (pp. 333-334).
- [narrative] He needed reinforcements to replace the units left at Antwerp, Brussels and along his communications; Moltke had sent none from the left wing (p. 334).
- [narrative] Moltke noticed the absence of prisoners, the usual sign of a rout, and stayed at headquarters fretting (p. 334).
- [narrative] Moltke's own envoy reported that the Moselle attack was not justifiable; Moltke agreed and did nothing (pp. 334-335).
- [interpretation] What decided Kluck was less the need to shorten his front than the belief that the French were already beaten (pp. 335-336).
- [interpretation] He did not rate the danger to his flank highly, because he thought Maunoury negligible and the French incapable of reinforcing him (pp. 335-336).
- [narrative] Bülow asked for the inward wheel on the evening of 30 August; OHL approved the same night as a way to close the gaps (p. 336).
- [narrative] The First Army had outrun its supply; the men marched some twenty-four miles a day, hungry and often drunk, sustained by the promise of Paris (pp. 337-338).
- [narrative] British rearguards held Kluck on 1 September and the BEF crossed the Marne on the 3rd (pp. 337, 345).
- [narrative] On 2 September Moltke ordered Kluck to follow in echelon as flank guard; Kluck crossed the Marne ahead of Bülow and left his two weakest units facing Paris (pp. 338-339).
- [narrative] GQG admitted on 31 August that its second plan, a stand on the Somme, had failed; its staff argued for retreat behind the Seine (pp. 339-340).
- [interpretation] Joffre did intend a general battle, but later and farther back than the one that occurred; he named 8 September and Brienne-le-Château (pp. 340-341).
- [narrative] The government left Paris for Bordeaux on the night of 2 September on Joffre's urging, although GQG by then had the captured map (pp. 343-344).
- [narrative] Gallieni thought the retreat to the Seine divorced from reality; he had not been told of the map (pp. 346-347).
- [narrative] On 3 September aviators of the Paris garrison saw the German columns moving east, and Gallieni's staff recognised the exposed flank (p. 347).

## Arguments

- **Premises**: (p. 334) no reinforcements from the left wing + (p. 334) detachments in Belgium + (p. 336) density below what an offensive required.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 336) the sweep round Paris was no longer possible with the men available, so both Kluck and Moltke welcomed the shorter line. The weakened right wing of ch02 and ch14 here forces the change.
- **Premises**: (pp. 335-336) German doctrine said field armies first + (p. 335) war games showed garrisons stay in their camps + (p. 335) the French seemed too broken to manoeuvre.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 336) the turn was the textbook answer. It was wrong because its premise about the French was wrong.
- **Premises**: (p. 340) Order No. 4 names the Paris garrison as a possible participant + (p. 346) the 2 September instruction directs it toward Meaux + (p. 346) GQG still looked to the Seine.
  **Conclusion**: (pp. 340-341) Joffre had the idea of a battle and Gallieni the sense of the moment. She gives each side of the controversy part of the credit.

## Evidence offered

- A French householder's description of Kluck (p. 333).
- Moltke's letter to his wife and Bauer's report on the Moselle front (pp. 334-335).
- A German officer's diary on exhaustion and drunkenness (pp. 337-338).
- The stone at Senlis naming the mayor and six hostages shot on 2 September (p. 338).
- The texts of Order No. 4 and the 2 September instruction (pp. 340, 346).
- Gallieni's own account of his preparations and his farewell to Millerand (pp. 342-344).
- Sourcing: I did not read this chapter's endnotes. In the text she says outright that every order of these four days is disputed between Joffre's and Gallieni's partisans (pp. 340-341) and that Bülow's motive for requesting the wheel is uncertain (p. 336). Kluck's reasoning is reconstructed mostly from his own account. Gallieni's scenes are in his own words.

## Tensions / links to other chapters

- The title answers ch02: the last man on the right was to brush the Channel (p. 32); he brushes Paris on the inside (p. 335).
- Moltke's refusal to stop the Moselle attack (p. 335) continues the Epinal decision (ch14, p. 199) and is the overcommitted left of the Afterword (af01, p. 366).
- The transfer of the IVth Corps from Verdun to Paris (pp. 341-342) is the reinforcement Kluck thought impossible, and the corps the taxis later carried (af01, p. 365).
- Kluck placed behind Bülow again (p. 338) repeats ch13 (pp. 191-192).
- Joffre's deft capture of the Paris command (p. 341) shows the calm of ch14 (p. 221) as political skill.
- Tension: the book condemned the German plan as rigid (ch02, p. 33) and here shows a German commander given the widest latitude, using it, and being wrong (p. 336).

## Open questions

- Was the turn Kluck's error or the consequence of a right wing already too weak? The chapter supports both (pp. 334, 335-336).
- Why did GQG let the government leave after it had the map (p. 343)? She offers uncertainty and the momentum of a decision already taken.
