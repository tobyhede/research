# ch09: "Home Before the Leaves Fall" (pp. 100–118)

## Question this chapter answers

How did Britain enter the war, why did Germany accept British hostility, and what did everyone assume about how long the war would last?

## Key terms

| Term | First used | Author's meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| War Book | pp. 100-101 | Britain's complete set of prepared orders for war; the method was ready and the will was not. |
| Naval pledge | p. 101 | Grey's written promise of 2 August to protect the French coast if the German fleet entered the Channel; a one-legged commitment. |
| Casus belli | p. 102 | The violation of Belgium, which Grey meant to make the formal ground for war because the real ground, German hegemony, had never been put to the country. |
| Grey's speech | pp. 103-105 | The 3 August statement to the Commons that carried the country by joining interest, honour and Belgium. |
| Short-war concept | p. 106 | The belief, from Clausewitz and from a misread Angell, that war must be quick; held by all sides, with six weeks to four months the usual estimates. |
| Kitchener's three years | p. 107 | The one prediction of a long war that was acted on. |
| Necessity knows no law | p. 114 | Bethmann's defence of the invasion to the Reichstag, with the admission that it was a wrong. |
| Scrap of paper | p. 115 | Bethmann's phrase to the British ambassador about the 1839 treaty. |
| Fear of invasion | p. 117 | The reason the British Cabinet hesitated to send the Regular Army abroad even after declaring war. |

## Leading propositions

- [interpretation] Britain's preparations were complete and its mind was muddled (pp. 100-101).
- [narrative] The Cabinet granted the naval pledge on 2 August at the cost of two resignations, with more threatened (p. 101).
- [interpretation] The policy of supporting France had been made behind closed doors, so neither government nor country would have gone to war united on it; Belgium was required (p. 102).
- [narrative] Belgium's refusal decided Lloyd George to stay (pp. 102-103).
- [narrative] Grey's speech won overwhelming support, with dissent from Labour and some Liberals (p. 105).
- [interpretation] Germany took the risk of Belgium because the General Staff had already counted Britain in and discounted her in a war expected to last four months (p. 106).
- [narrative] Germany had nitrates for six months; France left the Lorraine iron basin undefended and lost 80 per cent of its ore (pp. 106-107).
- [narrative] Moltke, Joffre and Kitchener each foresaw a long war; only Kitchener planned for one (p. 107).
- [interpretation] The Kaiser and his civilians refused to believe what they had been told about England; the soldiers took it as certain (pp. 107-108).
- [narrative] Germany declared war on France on 3 August, citing air raids that had not happened (p. 109).
- [narrative] German troops crossed into Belgium at Gemmerich at 8:02 on 4 August (p. 110).
- [interpretation] German leaders later recognised 4 August as the day they lost the first great battle in world opinion (p. 110).
- [narrative] Bethmann told the Reichstag the invasion was contrary to international law and would be made good (p. 114).
- [interpretation] The British ultimatum was sent late and in two parts because of an irresolute government (pp. 114-115).
- [narrative] Having declared war, the Cabinet disputed whether to send the Expeditionary Force at all; the order read mobilize, without embark (pp. 117-118).

## Arguments

- **Premises**: (p. 106) the war will be over in about four months + (p. 108) the BEF is small and expected.
  **Conclusion**: (pp. 106, 107) British entry does not matter, so Belgium is worth the price. The premise about duration is the one that failed.
- **Premises**: (p. 102) the public saw a Franco-German quarrel + (p. 102) Belgium was England's own treaty.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 102) only Belgium could unite the country, though France and the balance of power were the basic cause (p. 103).
- **Premises**: (p. 107) three military chiefs foresaw attrition + (p. 107) two of them planned for a short war anyway.
  **Conclusion**: restates the constant from ch02 (pp. 29-30): no one prepared for the harder alternative.
