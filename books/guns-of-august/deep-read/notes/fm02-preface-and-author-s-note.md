# fm02: Preface and Author's Note (pp. 7–12)

Two pieces by Tuchman: the Preface to the anniversary edition, written twenty-six years after publication (p. 11), and the original Author's Note on scope and method.

## Question this chapter answers

How did the book come to be limited to the first month of the war, what did the author leave out, and what standard of evidence does she claim?

## Key terms

| Term | First used | Author's meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The first month | p. 9 | Her chosen frame: the opening month of the war, which she says held all the roots of what followed. |
| Goeben | pp. 8-9 | The German warship whose escape to Constantinople brought Turkey into the war; her preferred subject and a family story. |
| Angel of Mons | p. 9 | The legend of supernatural help for the BEF at Mons; the subject her publisher wanted. |
| Rule on left and right | p. 12 | Rivers face downstream; armies keep the left and right they had when they set out, even in retreat. |
| "He must have" style | p. 12 | Invented attribution of thoughts to historical figures, which she says she avoided. |
| Documentary support | p. 12 | Her standard: every statement about weather, thoughts, feelings and states of mind rests on a document, cited in the Notes. |

## Leading propositions

- [narrative] The book grew from Bible and Sword and The Zimmermann Telegram, both centred on the First World War (pp. 7-8).
- [interpretation] 1914 was the date that ended the nineteenth century and began the present age (p. 8).
- [narrative] The publisher Cecil Scott of Macmillan wanted a book on Mons; she wanted the Goeben, which her family had witnessed when she was two (pp. 8-9).
- [narrative] She settled on the first month as a frame that contained both episodes and made the whole war manageable (p. 9).
- [narrative] She admits she never understood how the French regained part of Alsace at the start and wrote around it (pp. 9-10).
- [interpretation] She accepts Clifton Fadiman's statement of her thesis: the deadlock of August fixed the later course of the war, the peace terms, the inter-war period and the conditions of the second war (pp. 9-10).
- [interpretation] After twenty-six years she would change no passage, and she regards the closing paragraph of the Afterword as her best statement of the war's meaning (p. 10).
- [narrative] Austria-Hungary, Serbia and their fronts are omitted on purpose: the Balkans form a separate problem, and operations there reached no climax until Lemberg and the Drina, 8 to 17 September, outside her limits (p. 12).
- [narrative] All quoted remarks and all statements about weather and states of mind are sourced in the Notes (p. 12).

## Arguments

- **Premises**: (p. 9) the whole war was too large + (p. 9) the first month contained all the roots.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 9) a history of one month can stand for the origins of the war's shape.
- **Premises**: (p. 12) Balkan operations were preliminary in the first thirty-one days + (p. 12) including them would cost unity and length.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 12) the Austrian and Serbian fronts can be left out without loss. This is a choice about scope that also removes the place where the war began.

## Evidence offered

- Her own recollection of the commissioning; Fadiman's Book-of-the-Month Club review and the Publishers Weekly notice, quoted at length (pp. 9-11).
- Acknowledgments name the libraries used: New York Public Library, New York Society Library, the Hoover Library, the Imperial War Museum and the Bibliothèque de Documentation Internationale Contemporaine (pp. 11-12).
- Sourcing: she says she needed help with German (pp. 11-12) and describes long immersion in military memoirs (p. 12). Both bear on how the German side and the commanders' self-accounts are handled in the chapters.

## Quotes

- "the war’s first month, which contained all the roots" (p. 9)
- "to muffle the facts a bit when one can’t understand everything" (pp. 9-10)
- "There are no passages I would wish to change." (p. 10)
- "operations on the Austrian front during the first thirty-one days were purely preliminary" (p. 12)
- "states of mind public or private, in the following pages have documentary support" (p. 12)

## Open questions

- Documentary support for a state of mind often means a memoir written after the event. How far does the standard on p. 12 guard against self-justifying sources?
- Is a war that began over Serbia fairly explained with the Balkans left out?
