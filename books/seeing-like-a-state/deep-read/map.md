## The whole-book question

Why have so many large, well-intended, state-led schemes to improve the human condition failed, often catastrophically?

## Unity statement

Scott argues that states govern by simplifying society and nature into legible, standard forms, and that this capacity becomes disastrous when joined to a high-modernist faith in comprehensive rational design, an authoritarian state willing to impose it, and a civil society too weak to resist (pp. 9-10). The schemes fail because any planned order is a thin simplification that is parasitic on practical knowledge, metis, and informal process that it ignores or suppresses (p. 10; ch09, p. 243). He says he is not arguing against the state or planning as such, and that large-scale capitalism simplifies in the same way (pp. 11-12); the conclusion turns this into four planning rules and a case for institutions shaped by metis (ch10, pp. 273-274, 280).
