## Classification

- **Kind**: practical in aim. On Liberty and The Subjection of Women prescribe limits on social and legal power; Utilitarianism states and defends the standard by which Mill says such prescriptions are to be judged.
- **Genre**: moral and political philosophy in three essays, with an editor's introduction and a scholarly apparatus. Read with the philosophy strategy: dig for unstated premises, track shifting terms, and separate positions from the arguments for them.

## The whole-book question

By what standard should a society decide how far it may control its members, and what does that standard require for individual liberty and for the equality of the sexes?

## Unity statement

Mill holds that the general happiness is the standard of right, that happiness for beings like us consists largely in the free exercise and development of our own faculties, and that the rules protecting each person from harm and wrongful interference are the most binding part of morality. On Liberty applies this to the power of society over the individual and concludes that compulsion is justified only to prevent harm to others. The Subjection of Women applies it to the one remaining legal relation founded on birth and force and concludes that it must give way to perfect equality.

## High-value chapters for this reading

Ranked against the stated reading purpose: "Understand what Mill argues in each of the three works, how the arguments are built, how the works bear on one another, and where they are contestable."

1. ch01 — states the principle everything else in On Liberty qualifies, and its claim to rest on utility.
2. ch14 — the account of rights and justice that lets a utilitarian hold the positions of the other two works.
3. ch03 — the view of well-being as self-development on which the liberty argument depends.
4. ch06 — the method of The Subjection of Women: burden of proof, custom, and ignorance of nature.
5. ch11 and ch13 — what happiness is and why it is the standard; the weakest and most disputed steps.
6. ch02 — the most complete single argument in the volume.
7. ch09 — where liberty, equality and happiness are explicitly joined.

## Mode and plan

- **Mode chosen**: study.
- **Plan**: every unit read in full and given an analytical note. fm01 and ch01-ch14, ap01 and ap02 were read and noted in one sequential pass by a single reader. ap03 was read and noted by a second reader in a separate context after the first ran out of room. Order of reading: On Liberty, Utilitarianism, The Subjection of Women, then the commentary.
