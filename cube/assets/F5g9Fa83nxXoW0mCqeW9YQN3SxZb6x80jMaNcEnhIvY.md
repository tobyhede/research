| Term | First used | Author's meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Economic motive | p. 121 | Strictly, does not exist. What is so called is "the desire for general opportunity, the desire for power to achieve unspecified ends". Only "economic factors conditioning our striving for other ends" exist. |
| Economic ends | p. 121 | None, apart from "the pathological case of the miser". Ultimate ends of reasonable beings "are never economic". |
| Money | p. 122 | "One of the greatest instruments of freedom ever invented by man": a reward in a form that leaves the recipient to choose how to enjoy it. Hated only because it is the medium through which scarcity is felt. |
| Economic gain / loss ("merely" economic) | p. 122 | A gain or loss "where it is still in our power to decide which of our needs or desires shall be affected"; in a free economy it falls on the margin, on the least important wants. This is true "only in such a free economy". |
| Economic control | p. 123 | Not control of a separable sector but "the control of the means for all our ends". |
| Central planning | p. 123 | The economic problem "solved by the community instead of by the individual", so the community's representatives decide the relative importance of needs. Here always a single authority "directing all economic activity" (p. 123), "the whole economic system" (p. 124). |
| Economic freedom (two senses) | p. 123, p. 129 | (a) The planners' sense: "freedom from economic care", being relieved of the need and the power to choose (pp. 123, 129). (b) Hayek's sense: "the freedom of our economic activity which, with the right of choice, inevitably also carries the risk and the responsibility of that right" (p. 129). Only (b) is the prerequisite of other freedoms. |
| Power (of the planner) | p. 124 | Power over consumption arising from control of production; that of "the most powerful monopolist conceivable", with complete power to decide what we are given and on what terms. |
| Price (in a competitive society) | p. 124 | A rate reflecting what others must forgo; "not determined by the conscious will of anybody". An obstacle that expresses competing wants, not someone's disapproval. |
| Free choice of occupation | p. 125 | Not abundance of options but having "some choice", not being absolutely tied, with a way out at the price of some sacrifice. |
| Cash nexus | pp. 126-127 | The fact that most things can be had at a price; defended as what lets individuals decide their own trade-offs. |
| Economic problem | p. 127 | The necessity of choosing because "all our ends compete for the same means". What people resent, he says, is that this problem exists at all. |
| Potential plenty | p. 127 | The claim that scarcity is already abolished and only distribution remains; "palpably untrue". |
| Laissez faire | p. 128 | Used once as a period label ("the age of laissez faire"), not as a statement of principle; compare ch06, p. 114, where the term is called misleading. |
