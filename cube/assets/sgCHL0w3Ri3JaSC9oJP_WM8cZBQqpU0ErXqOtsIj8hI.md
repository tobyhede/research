- **Author's claim:** when r is well above g for a long time, inheritance comes to dominate saving. [Ch 11, line 11442](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L11442) In France the yearly inheritance flow went from 20–25% of national income in the nineteenth century to 4–5% in the 1950s and about 15% in 2010. [Ch 11, line 11498](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L11498)
- **Reasoning:** the flow equals μ × m × β: the wealth of the dying relative to the living, times the death rate, times the capital/income ratio. Longer lives do not end inheritance, because wealth ages with its owners. [Ch 11, line 11606](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L11606) [Ch 11, line 11779](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L11779)
- **Evidence:** inherited wealth was 80–90% of private wealth in the nineteenth century, about 40% at its low in the 1970s and about two-thirds in 2010. [Ch 11, line 12144](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L12144) For cohorts born from the 1970s on, top inheritors and top earners are roughly level again, a reversal of the twentieth-century pattern (Rastignac's dilemma). [Ch 11, line 12365](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L12365)
- **Boundary:** the evidence is strongest for France. The data for Britain, Germany and the US are weaker, and the projections are “highly uncertain”. [Ch 11, line 12856](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L12856) [Ch 11, line 12059](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L12059)
- **Application [Application]:** in debates about inheritance tax, separate the size of the yearly flow from the share of wealth that was inherited.
