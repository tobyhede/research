- How damaging are the editor's corrections to the Carr indictment? The society/state quotation (p. 203) is the basis of the Schmitt comparison; if Carr was reporting a belief, the "accidental or deliberate" insinuation (p. 193) fails. Synthesis should treat Hayek's quotations of opponents in this chapter as paraphrase-grade unless checked.
- Is "resemblance of ideas" evidence of a causal tendency, or is this the genetic fallacy? Test against ch. 12's method.
- Did the post-1945 Labour government's nationalizations and retained controls produce the predicted results? Hayek's prediction is conditional and undated ("if we continue") (p. 198); synthesis should state what would count as falsification, and compare with the 1956 and 1976 prefaces.
- What distinguishes an "inevitable" monopoly from a merely organized one, and who decides? Hayek gives hypothetical examples only (p. 199).
- Is stringent price control of a private monopoly (p. 200) administrable under the Rule of Law as defined in ch. 6, given that setting a price that "leaves no room for extraordinary profits" requires discretionary judgment?
- The claim that monopoly wages impoverish "still more all other wage-earners" (p. 199) is asserted without mechanism here; where, if anywhere, is it argued?
- Is the equation of English "organization"/planning with German ideas fair to non-German sources of English collectivism (Fabianism, Benthamite administration, wartime experience)? Hayek's own footnote dates the "German" atmosphere in England to men from the First World War controls (p. 206), which suggests a domestic, experiential source.
- How does Hayek's low alarm about the traditionalist Right (p. 201) square with the fact that the totalitarian regime under discussion came to power with conservative assistance?
