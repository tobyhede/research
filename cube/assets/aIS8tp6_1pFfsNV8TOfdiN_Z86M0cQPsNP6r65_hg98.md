| Term | First used | Author's meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "End of Economic Man" | p. 207 | Drucker's phrase, treated as a governing myth: the claim that people now weigh economic considerations less. Hayek calls it "doubly misleading" because it describes a past that never existed and a direction not being taken (p. 208). |
| Economophobia | p. 208 | Hayek's coinage for the actual attitude: not contempt for material welfare but refusal to recognize any obstacle or conflict of aims that economic argument reveals; impatience with all restraint on immediate ambitions (p. 208). |
| Impersonal forces (of the market) | p. 208 | Forces that coordinate individual efforts in a complex society and that no one can fully understand; they are "seemingly irrational" and are not "the conscious decisions of an intelligent being" (p. 209). Individuals must "submit" to them. They embody facts "no individual can completely survey" (p. 209). Compared with ch. 13's "impersonal discipline of the market" (p. 201), the term gains this epistemic content and a quasi-religious register (submission, humility, awe). |
| Incomplete (erroneous) rationalism | p. 209 | The refusal to yield to anything not understood or not consciously designed; "incomplete" because it does not grasp that coordination in a complex society must use facts no single mind can survey (p. 209). |
| Single purpose | p. 210 | An end to which the whole society is "entirely and permanently subordinated"; incompatible with individual freedom except in war and other temporary disasters (p. 210). |
| Full employment | p. 210 | A "vague but popular" phrase. Hayek distinguishes the legitimate goal ("the conquest of unemployment") from the policy of securing the short-run maximum of employment by keeping everyone employed where they happen to be through monetary expansion (pp. 210-211). He rejects the second, and the slogan "at any price". |
| Maximum of employment achievable by monetary means | p. 211 | The short-run maximum got by employing people where they are; sustainable only by progressive inflation and at the cost of blocking the redistribution of labor between industries (pp. 211-212). |
| Coercion | p. 211 | Here specific: selecting individuals for compulsory transfer to less well paid jobs (p. 211). |
| Uniform minimum / basic minimum | p. 213 | A minimum income secured to everybody, accepted; contrasted with "privileged security of particular classes", which must lapse (p. 213). Same distinction as ch. 9's two kinds of security. |
| Morals | p. 213 | "A phenomenon of individual conduct", existing only where the individual is free to decide and is "called upon voluntarily to sacrifice personal advantage to the observance of a moral rule" (p. 213). Essence: responsibility to one's own conscience, duty not exacted by compulsion, bearing the consequences of one's choices (p. 214). No moral merit attaches to compelled good conduct or to unselfishness at another's expense (pp. 213-214). |
| Responsibility | p. 213 | Being answerable for one's own interests and free to sacrifice them; owed "not to a superior, but to one's conscience" (p. 214). Collectivism is defined morally as "a movement whose main promise is the relief from responsibility" (p. 214). |
| Collectivism (moral effect) | p. 213 | Not only an economic system but a fifty-year movement that replaces individual virtues with "the demand for obedience" and has "inherently centralistic tendencies" (pp. 215, 216). |
| Individualist virtues | p. 214 | Independence, self-reliance, willingness to bear risk, readiness to back one's conviction against a majority, voluntary cooperation (p. 214); extended on p. 216 to local responsibility, non-interference, tolerance of the different and queer, respect for custom and tradition, suspicion of power. |
| Liberalism (English) | p. 216 | Not party Liberalism but the shared set of ideas held "irrespective of party" by almost all Englishmen until about twenty years before (p. 216). |
| Monster state | p. 218 | What Germans and Italians who have learned the lesson want protection against; contrasted with "their own little worlds" (p. 218). |
