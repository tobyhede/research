- **National balance sheets**, eight richest countries 1970–2010, annual (Figures 5.3, 5.5, 5.7) and Europe/US 1870–2010 decennial (Figures 5.1-5.2). Europe is the income-weighted average of Germany, France, Britain and Italy (note 1, p. 446). Sources are stated as national accounts following "international standards" (pp. 128-129) and the online appendix; all figures cite only "piketty.pse.ens.fr/capital21c".
- **Tables 5.1-5.4** (growth and saving rates; components of private saving; gross and net saving; public saving) are referred to (pp. 125-128, 133) but did not survive extraction. Their key values are restated in the text: per capita growth 1.6–2.0 percent, population growth under 0.5 vs 1.0–1.5 percent (p. 125); saving 7–15 percent (p. 126); depreciation 10–15 percent of national income (p. 128).
- **Savings-flow simulation**: predicted vs actual 2010 private wealth by country (p. 126; Supplemental Figure S5.1, note 9, p. 447); worked Japanese figures (note 10, p. 447). Britain is the stated misfit (p. 126), explained conjecturally by undervalued public assets in the 1970s and cheap privatisations (p. 133).
- **1970 and 2010 ranges**: 2.2 years (Germany) to 3.4 (US) in 1970; 4.1 (Germany, US) to 6.1 (Japan) and 6.8 (Italy) in 2010 (notes 6-7, p. 446).
- **Durables and valuables**: national-accounts estimates for 1970–2010 and Gregory King's estimates for Britain around 1700 (p. 129); gold's share of private wealth (note 14, p. 447).
- **Foundations and church property**: France 1 percent, Japan 3–4 percent, US 6–7 percent of private wealth; eighteenth-century French church property 7–8 percent of private wealth (p. 131), source in the online appendix (note 17, p. 448).
- **Tobin's Q**: Figure 5.6; CAC 40 firms ranged from 20 percent to over 340 percent in 2012, from an accounting firm report (p. 136; note 23, p. 448). National Q: US 100–120 percent, Britain 120–140 percent, France about 80 percent, Germany and Japan 50–70 percent (pp. 136-137).
- **Former Soviet bloc**: "highly imperfect estimates" of private wealth of about four years now and public capital of three to four years before 1990 (p. 133).
- **World β 1870–2100**: Europe and North America reliable from 1870; Japan from incomplete data including probate records from 1905 (p. 140); the rest of the world (just over a fifth of global output) estimated from 1990 at about four years and *assumed* constant back to 1870 (p. 140). The projection uses ch02's growth scenario and an assumed s of 10 percent (p. 140).
- **Pure land**: contemporary eighteenth-century estimates of improvements (p. 141), Paine's *Agrarian Justice* (1795) (p. 141), Goldsmith on ancient Rome (note 33, p. 450). Piketty's own estimate of urban pure land, half to one year of national income, comes with "substantial" uncertainty (p. 142).
- **Gross financial positions**: central bank and statistical agency balance sheets of primary financial assets, excluding derivatives (p. 139; note 28, p. 449).
