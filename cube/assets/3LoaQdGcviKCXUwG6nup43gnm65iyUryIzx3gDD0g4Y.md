How and why did the structure of income inequality change in France and the United States between the Belle Époque and 2010? Piketty's answer has two halves. In France (standing in for continental Europe and Japan) total income inequality fell because top incomes from capital collapsed between 1914 and 1945, while the wage hierarchy stayed almost unchanged: "rentiers fell behind managers". In the United States inequality fell less in 1914–1945, then rose after 1980 to Belle Époque European levels, and this rise is mostly a rise in *wage* inequality at the very top: the arrival of "supermanagers". The chapter also sets out, more openly than most, the limits of the income-tax sources on which the whole of Part Three rests.
