- **Author's claim:** the drift to monopoly and planning comes from opinion and policy, not from technology. The more complex an economy, the less any one mind can survey it, and the more it depends on prices to tell each person what he needs to know. [Ch 4, line 1172](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/road-to-serfdom/source/source.txt#L1172) [Ch 4, line 1192](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/road-to-serfdom/source/source.txt#L1192) [Ch 4, line 1194](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/road-to-serfdom/source/source.txt#L1194)
- **Reasoning:** he takes three versions of the inevitability claim (economies of scale, complexity, new technology needing protection) and answers each.
- **Evidence:** the strongest data in the book: a 1941 US congressional inquiry finding that large-scale efficiency had not been shown to destroy competition, plus the timing of cartels in Germany and of monopoly in Britain after the 1931 tariff. - **Boundary:** he grants that monopoly has grown, that towns and utilities are not handled well by competition, and that planned societies have produced some good things.
