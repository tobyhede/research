| Term | First used | Author's meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| freedom (liberal sense) | p. 74 | Freedom from coercion, from the arbitrary power of other men; release from ties that leave the individual no choice but obedience to a superior. A relation between persons. |
| 'new freedom' / 'economic freedom' (socialist sense) | pp. 74-75 | Freedom from necessity: release from the compulsion of circumstances that limit everyone's range of choice. Hayek says this is merely another name for power or wealth, and in practice another name for the old demand for equal distribution of wealth. |
| economic freedom (shift) | pp. 74-75 | Here the phrase belongs to the socialists and means freedom from want. In ch. 1 the near-identical phrase 'freedom in economic affairs' (p. 65) was Hayek's own and meant freedom of economic activity. Hayek explicitly distinguishes freedom from coercion from freedom from necessity, explaining that socialists and liberals use the word freedom in different senses. |
| socialism | p. 74 | Historically: a doctrine that began as a reaction against the liberalism of the French Revolution, calling for deliberate reorganisation of society on hierarchical lines under a coercive 'spiritual power'. Still not defined analytically see ch. 3 (p. 80). |
| democratic socialism | p. 74, p. 78 | Socialism allied, since before 1848, with the forces of freedom; 'the great utopia of the last few generations', held to be unachievable. |
| liberalism / 'liberal of the old type' | p. 74, p. 77 | The doctrine socialism displaced among progressives; the one who 'really believes in individual freedom' and is the real enemy of both communist and Nazi. |
| collectivism | p. 74 | Used once as the general name for what the liberal thinkers warned against; not defined. |
| democracy | p. 74 | Via Tocqueville: 'an essentially individualist institution' in irreconcilable conflict with socialism. |
| planners / planning boards | p. 74 | Saint-Simon is 'the first of modern planners'; the word is used without definition. |
