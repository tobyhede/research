- **Mechanism argument**: A1 (pp. 168-172). No empirical support at the point of argument; the examples come after.
- **Illustrative psychological sketch**: the leader who dislikes Jews and admires the tall blond figure of the novels of his youth and so embraces a pseudoscientific theory (p. 170). A transparent portrait of Hitler; conjectural psychology, not evidence.
- **Historical example**: Blut und Boden as a myth combining values with causal beliefs, including a "probably erroneous" idea about country people as soldiers (p. 170). Editor's note explains the doctrine and attributes the term's introduction to Spengler (p. 177, note 3).
- **Appeal to theorists**: Plato's noble lies, Sorel's myths, Nazi racial doctrine, Mussolini's corporative state as serving the same purpose (p. 171). Editor glosses Plato and Sorel (p. 177, note 4). The assimilation of Plato to modern totalitarian theorists is asserted.
- **Quotation from an opponent**: Mannheim on planned freedom and on older conceptions of freedom as an obstacle (p. 171). **Editor's correction**: Hayek gave p. 377; the passage is on p. 379 (p. 177, note 6). The quoted sentence carries a bracketed sic after the word all (p. 171). My observation: Mannheim's sentence says earlier freedoms need not all be abolished; Hayek reads the reassurance as an admission. This is interpretation, and a hostile one.
- **Quotation from an ally**: Drucker on the new freedom (p. 177, note 7, Hayek's note). **Editor's correction**: the first part is on p. 79 and the last on p. 80 of Drucker, not p. 74 (p. 177, note 7). Note the Drucker quotation as Hayek gives it contains an ellipsis.
- **Title allusion**: Becker's New Liberties for Old (p. 171; p. 177, note 5).
- **Personal testimony**: one must have experienced it; the two brothers who can no longer communicate (p. 172); the climate is something one must personally experience (p. 175). Anecdote from his Austrian experience; not checkable.
- **Quotation from sympathizers reporting on Russia**: the Webbs on public expression of doubt as disloyalty in Russian enterprises (p. 172; p. 177, note 8). Strong evidence of its kind because the witnesses were admirers. But note it concerns doubts voiced while work is in progress within an enterprise; Hayek extends it to the whole social plan by inference (p. 172).
- **Assertion with appeal to experience**: history, law and economics have become factories of official myth in all totalitarian countries; "amply confirmed by experience" (p. 173). No instance cited.
- **Paired quotations, unsourced by Hayek**: attacks on relativity as Semitic and as contrary to dialectical materialism; attacks on mathematical statistics; continuity and bourgeois prejudice (pp. 173-174). No citations are given for these, by Hayek or the editor.
- **Quotation via the Webbs**: Soviet journal slogans, party in mathematics and purity of theory in surgery (p. 174; the editor supplies the page reference, p. 177, note 9).
- **German parallel**: the journal of the National-Socialist mathematicians; Lenard's German Physics (p. 174). Editor identifies Lenard and the work (p. 177, note 10).
- **Quotation, deliberately unattributed**: the chess exhortation, with the reader left to guess Germany or Russia (p. 174). **Editor's identification**: Krylenko, Soviet Commissar for Justice, 1932, cited in Souvarine (p. 177, note 11). The rhetorical device presumes interchangeability, which is the thing to be shown.
- **Quotation, attribution uncertain**: the Nazi minister of justice on scientific theories serving National Socialism (p. 174). **Editor's note**: it is not clear which of three ministers said it (pp. 177-178, note 12). Hayek gives no source.
- **Quotation from a British opponent**: J. G. Crowther defending the Inquisition as beneficial to science when it protects a rising class (p. 175; p. 178, note 13). Hayek calls this practically indistinguishable from the views that led to Nazi persecution of scientists (p. 175); a strong equation resting on one sentence.
- **Confident language outrunning evidence**: "everywhere among intellectuals who have embraced a collectivist faith" (p. 175); "equally abhorrent to the Nazis, our socialist intellectuals, and the communists" (p. 174); the oppression "openly advocated by people who pretend to speak for the scientists of liberal countries" (p. 175), with Crowther the only name.
- **Conceptual argument**: A5 on the growth of reason (p. 176). No evidence needed or offered; it is a philosophical claim.
