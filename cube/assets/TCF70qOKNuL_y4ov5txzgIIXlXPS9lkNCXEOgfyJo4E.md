### Closing footnotes (pp. 36-37), added after the first pass

These sit past the unit boundary, on the page where Hayek's own preface begins. All are Caldwell's.

- **Footnote 101** (pp. 36-37): the 1976 denial of the slippery-slope reading is in this volume's 1976 preface. Caldwell adds that Hayek was wrong to imply Samuelson originated the misreading, since it was a common one, and that the archive holds Samuelson's reply apologising and promising to represent Hayek more accurately in future (p. 37).
- **Footnote 103** (p. 37): source for the remark that Hayek was not against government activity as such: his address to the Economic Club of Detroit, p. 4 of the printed address.
- **Footnote 104** (p. 37): the line about methods most socialists would disapprove of is cross-referenced to chapter 10 of this volume.
- **Footnote 105** (p. 37): source for the statement of the logical argument: the 1939 pamphlet version of Freedom and the Economic System, p. 205. **[My observation]** Footnotes 107 and 108 confirm that the war-and-state-power theme is Caldwell's 2005 application, drawn from Horwitz and Higgs, not from Hayek's text. Footnote 105's Arrow comparison is the one substantive idea here: it bears on the chapter 5 claim that no agreed ranking of ends exists, and is worth following up in a cross-book synthesis. Footnote 102 weakens the use of the Samuelson letter as evidence that the inevitability reading was a careless one, since Caldwell concedes the reading was widespread.
