- **Author's claim:** in every country and period with data, the top 10% of earners take 25–30% of labor income, while the top 10% of wealth holders own over 50%, sometimes 90%. The bottom half owns under 10% of wealth, usually under 5%. [Ch 7, line 7428](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L7428)
- **Reasoning:** neither precautionary nor life-cycle saving can explain this, because wealth is almost as concentrated within each age group as across the whole population. Inheritance and the compounding of returns explain it. [Ch 7, line 7467](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L7467) [Ch 7, line 7494](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L7494)
- **Evidence:** in France in 2010–2011 the top 10% held 62% of wealth and the bottom half 4%. In 1900–1910 the top 10% held about 90%, and the middle 40% only 5–10%. [Ch 7, line 7793](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L7793) [Ch 7, line 7915](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L7915)
- **Boundary:** the figures are rounded on purpose, and the class labels are explicitly arbitrary. Piketty also argues that Gini coefficients and P90/P10 ratios hide the top of the distribution. [Ch 7, line 7515](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L7515) [Ch 7, line 8139](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L8139) **[Analysis]** The claim about within-age concentration is supported only by an endnote.
- **Application [Application]:** ask any inequality statistic whether it covers wealth or income, and whether it shows the top 1%.
