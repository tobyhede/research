Piketty assembles tax, estate and national-accounts series for more than twenty countries over three centuries to show that the capital/income ratio and wealth concentration followed a U-shape: high in the Belle Époque, collapsed by the shocks of 1914–1945, and recovering since the 1970s. He explains the pattern with β = s/g and α = r × β and argues that when the return on capital r exceeds growth g, as it did before 1914 and likely will again as growth slows, inherited wealth comes to dominate and inequality becomes arbitrary and incompatible with meritocratic democracy. His remedy is a progressive global tax on capital, backed by financial transparency and regional political integration, alongside a modernised social state and progressive income tax.
