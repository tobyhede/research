- **Depends on ch05**: the plan implies a complete ethical code that does not exist and cannot be settled democratically (recalled at pp. 169-170, "we have seen").
- **Depends on ch02**: freedom confused with power (p. 171) is the "new freedom" argument of The Great Utopia; here it is recast as a propaganda technique rather than an intellectual mistake. Shift: in ch02 the redefinition is a seductive error of socialists; here it is "perversion" used as an instrument (p. 171).
- **Depends on ch06 and ch08**: justice, law, right and equality are said to have already been shown to suffer the same reversal (p. 172).
- **Depends on ch10**: collectivist morals (p. 169) and the "not accidental by-products" formula (p. 174; ch10 p. 154). The parallel between individual conscience as arbiter of moral rules (ch10 p. 161) and as arbiter of evidence (p. 174) is deliberate.
- **Extends ch04 and ch07**: the knowledge argument for the market (dispersed knowledge, unpredictable results, competition as discovery) is here transposed to intellectual life (p. 176). The final paragraph ties the whole book's individualism to an epistemic stance, not only a moral one. This is the bridge to Hayek's later work; my observation.
- **Feeds ch12**: German philosophers and theoreticians of socialism prepared the perversion of freedom (p. 171).
- **Feeds ch13**: collectivists among our scientists and British intellectuals (pp. 173, 175).
- **Tension** (mine): Hayek concedes that most people take their views ready-made and that free thought matters directly only to a small minority (p. 175), which sits beside ch10's lowest-common-denominator passage (p. 156). His liberalism is not grounded in confidence in mass judgement but in the unplannability of progress. Compare with any democratic-theory material in ch05.
- **Tension** (mine): he says conformity on values is normal in any community (p. 169), yet ch05 and ch10 rest on the claim that no agreed value scale exists in a free society. The two are consistent only if communities share general moral rules but not a ranking of concrete ends.
- **Shift in "propaganda"**: neutral in principle at p. 168 and p. 176 (ideas are tested by argument and propaganda), pejorative when monopolized. Compare ch09 p. 151, where anticapitalist propaganda in a free society is blamed for changing the values of the young; there plural propaganda is already treated as harmful.
- **Slide of scope** (mine): the argument is framed for a totalitarian system (p. 168) but the conclusion is applied to "a planned or totalitarian system" (p. 174). As in ch10, the force against democratic planning depends on the earlier chapters' claim that comprehensive planning becomes totalitarian.
