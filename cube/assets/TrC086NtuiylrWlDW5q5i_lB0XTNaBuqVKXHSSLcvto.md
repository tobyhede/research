- **Citation in place of argument**: Robbins, "Economic Planning and International Order", "passim", for the whole first section (p. 233); Hayek says the points "can be touched upon only very briefly".
- **Bare assertion with "needs little emphasis"**: the incompatibility of unrestricted national economic policy with peace (p. 221). The interwar trade restrictions are alluded to ("only too visible before 1939") but not described.

- **Historical example, in a footnote**: British colonial development involves imposing values on those assisted, and has made colonial experts skeptical of international colonial administration (p. 233).
- **Contemporary opinion, unsourced**: English people arguing that reconstruction of Europe should serve British industry (pp. 223-224).

**Editor's corrections of Hayek's quotations and citations**

- Epigraph: Acton wrote "federalism", not "federation" (p. 233).
- Sidgwick wrote "some further integration", not "some future integration" (p. 234).
- Hayek's note 13 gives Robbins's title as "Economic Planning and Economic Order", whereas note 2 gives "Economic Planning and International Order" (pp. 233, 234); the editor does not flag this.
- The editor identifies Tennyson's poem as "Locksley Hall" and quotes the lines (p. 234), and records that both Hayek and Robbins favoured European federation (p. 234).
- All slips are minor and none changes sense; contrast ch. 13, where one omission bears on the charge against Carr.
