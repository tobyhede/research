- The claim is conditional. Hayek writes in the Introduction that the outcome can be prevented, and in 1976 he denies ever saying that any step toward socialism must end in totalitarianism. [Introduction, line 934](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/road-to-serfdom/source/source.txt#L934) [1976 preface, line 900](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/road-to-serfdom/source/source.txt#L900)
- The target is central direction of economic activity under one plan, which is how Hayek says socialism was understood in 1944. It is not government activity as such. [Ch 3, line 1128](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/road-to-serfdom/source/source.txt#L1128) [1976 preface, line 898](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/road-to-serfdom/source/source.txt#L898)
- The book tells you how to judge an intervention (is it a general rule, known in advance, not aimed at particular people?) but not where the line falls. Keynes, both of the publisher's readers, and Hayek himself all say so. [Caldwell, line 436](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/road-to-serfdom/source/source.txt#L436) [Ch 16, line 2420](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/road-to-serfdom/source/source.txt#L2420)
