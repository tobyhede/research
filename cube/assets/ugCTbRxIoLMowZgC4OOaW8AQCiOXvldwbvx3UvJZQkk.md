What form of international order can secure peace after the war without reproducing, on a larger scale, the dangers of planning? Hayek's answer: neither independent national planning (which produces conflict between states) nor international economic planning (which can only be rule by force of the strongest), but a federation: a supernational political authority with strictly limited, mainly negative powers, bound by the Rule of Law, able to forbid states to harm one another and to restrict trade, and not empowered to direct their economic life (pp. 221-223, 228-232).
