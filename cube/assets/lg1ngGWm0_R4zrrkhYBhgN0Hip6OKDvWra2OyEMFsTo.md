Economic security is widely held to be a precondition of real liberty. In what sense is that true, and at what point does the pursuit of security become a threat to liberty? Hayek's answer turns on splitting "security" into two kinds: one the state may and should provide, one it cannot provide to all without abolishing free choice of occupation.

Epigraphs (chosen by Hayek, sourced by the editor): Lenin 1917 on society as a single office and factory, and Trotsky 1937 on the sole-employer state, where the old rule that who does not work shall not eat becomes who does not obey shall not eat (p. 143; editor's sources at p. 153, notes 1-2).
