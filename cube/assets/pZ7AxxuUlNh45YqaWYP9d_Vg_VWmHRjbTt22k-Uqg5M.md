**Larger capital means a larger capital share.** α = r × β is an identity. The return on capital falls only slowly as capital grows, because capital and labor substitute easily (elasticity above one). So the capital share rises with β. - **Author's claim:** capital's share of income equals the return times the capital/income ratio. This is “a pure accounting identity.” [Ch 1, line 1796](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L1796) Whether a larger β raises α depends on how fast r falls as capital accumulates. [Ch 6, line 6614](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L6614)
- **Reasoning:** if capital substitutes easily for labor (elasticity above one), r falls less than β rises, and α grows. Piketty puts the historical elasticity at 1.3–1.6 and concludes that no self-correcting mechanism stops β and α rising together. [Ch 6, line 6769](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L6769) [Ch 6, line 6804](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L6804)
- **Evidence:** in Britain and France the capital share was 35–40% in the nineteenth century, 20–25% in the mid-twentieth and 25–30% now. The “pure” return oscillated around 4–5% with no long-run trend. [Ch 6, line 6112](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L6112) [Ch 6, line 6280](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L6280) Across rich countries the capital share rose from 15–25% in 1970 to 25–30% in 2000–2010. [Ch 6, line 6807](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L6807)
- **Boundary:** Piketty admits the elasticity estimate is uncertain. The same data fit a rise in capital's bargaining power. [Ch 6, line 6786](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L6786) [Ch 6, line 6798](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L6798) **[Analysis]** β is measured at market value, so asset-price booms raise β and lower measured r without any change in physical capital. The elasticity argument treats β as a volume.
- **Application [Application]:** when someone says labor's share is naturally stable, ask what is happening to the capital stock and how quickly returns are falling.
