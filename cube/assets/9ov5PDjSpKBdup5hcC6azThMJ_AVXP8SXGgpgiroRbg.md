- **National balance sheets** for Germany 1870–2010, the US 1770–2010 and Canada 1860–2010 (Figures 4.1-4.11). All cite only "piketty.pse.ens.fr/capital21c"; figures show decennial averages only (note 1, p. 444).
- **Germany**: data "more diverse" because of late unification and territorial changes (p. 101). Inflation of nearly 17 percent a year with prices multiplied by 300 (p. 103); note 2 (p. 444) says this omits 1923, when prices rose by a factor of 100 million. Public debt above 100 percent of GDP in 1918–1920 and 150 percent in 1943–1944 (p. 103). Lower Saxony holds more than 15 percent of Volkswagen shares and 20 percent of votes (p. 103).
- **Europe-wide**: Britain, France and Germany were more than two-thirds of Western European GDP in 1910 and 2010 (p. 105); estimates for Italy, Spain, Belgium, the Netherlands and Austria are referred to the appendix (note 7, p. 445).
- **War destruction and decomposition**: stated magnitudes (p. 106) and shares (p. 108); details in the online appendix (note 9, p. 445).
- **United States**: national capital "based on numerous contemporary estimates I have collected and compared" (p. 108); land prices confirmed by "probate records and wills" (p. 109); Tocqueville (1840) on scarce capital and cheap land (p. 109, note 10, p. 445). Net foreign position: 10 percent of national income in 1913 against 500 percent domestic capital (p. 111); about 20 percent against 450 percent today (p. 112). The better return on US foreign assets than on US liabilities is attributed to confidence in the dollar (p. 112).
- **Canada**: domestic capital 530 percent and net foreign-owned 120 percent of national income in 1910 (p. 113); 410 percent and under 10 percent today (p. 113).
- **Slavery**: Alice Hanson Jones's probate records, Raymond Goldsmith's tax and census data, Robert Fogel's slave-market transactions, described as "quite consistent with one another" (p. 114). Census counts of slaves and population (p. 114). 1860 prices: about $2,000 for a prime-age male slave against $200 annual wage for a free farm labourer (p. 117). Details in the online appendix (note 17, p. 445).
- **Cultural illustration**: *Titanic*, Henry James, *Mad Men*, *Django Unchained* (pp. 110, 112, 117). These carry no evidential weight.
- **Authorities**: Lenin's *Imperialism* (p. 102); Michel Albert's *Capitalisme contre capitalisme* on varieties of capitalism (note 5, p. 445); Samuelson's textbook on stable capital/output ratios (p. 111); Mira Wilkins on reactions to foreign investment in the US (note 13, p. 445); World Bank "wealth of nations" reports (p. 117, unnamed).
