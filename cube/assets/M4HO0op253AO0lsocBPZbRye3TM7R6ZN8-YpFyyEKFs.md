How fast has the world economy grown since antiquity, split into population growth and growth of output per head, and how fast is it likely to grow in the twenty-first century? Why does the answer matter for inequality? Piketty's answer: growth has "always been relatively slow except in exceptional periods or when catch-up is occurring" (p. 55); at the technological frontier, per capita growth has never exceeded about 1.5% for long; population growth is heading back toward zero; so g will likely fall, which strengthens inherited wealth and makes r > g more likely. The chapter also argues that 1% growth is fast in generational terms, that single price indices mislead over long periods, and that the stable money of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, ended by World War I, underpinned the rentier society.
