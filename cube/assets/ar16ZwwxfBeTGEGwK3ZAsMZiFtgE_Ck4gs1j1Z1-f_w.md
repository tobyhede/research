- **The self-inflicted-crisis argument.**
  **Premises**: (p. 64) the pre-totalitarian history of Germany, Italy and Russia shows few features unfamiliar to us; (p. 64) for a generation the democracies have been guided by the same ideals, which have already transformed them; (p. 64) no change in the facts compelled the change of ideas; (unstated) the ideals themselves, not the special circumstances of those countries, are what produced totalitarianism there.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 63) the present state of the world may be the result of genuine error on our own part, and (p. 64) winning the war only buys another chance to face the basic problems.

- **Economic freedom as a condition of other freedoms.**
  **Premises**: (p. 65) personal and political freedom has never existed in the past without freedom in economic affairs; (p. 65) we have progressively abandoned economic freedom; (p. 65) a new form of slavery has arisen before our eyes; (unstated) what has never existed without a condition cannot exist without it, that is, the historical correlation is a necessary connection.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 65) the abandonment of economic freedom and the new slavery are connected, as Tocqueville and Acton warned. The chapter gives the historical correlation only; the mechanism is left to chs. 5-9.

- **Civilisation-as-individualism argument.**
  **Premises**: (p. 65) the essential feature of Western civilisation since the Renaissance is individualism; (p. 66) its spread tracked commerce and the absence of despotic power; (pp. 66-67) science and material progress followed the unchaining of individual energies and were stifled where majority belief bound everyone; (p. 67) the results surpassed all expectations and benefited every class; (unstated) the correlation of freedom with progress is causal and would hold in future.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 69) the present trend is 'an entire abandonment of the individualist tradition which has created Western civilization', so what is at stake is the source of the progress people take for granted.

- **Why liberalism declined (an internal diagnosis).**
  **Premises**: (pp. 67-68) liberals hardened a strong presumption into an exceptionless rule, laissez faire, so that one breach brought down the whole position; (p. 68) improvement of the institutional framework was necessarily slow because it depended on growing understanding; (p. 68) liberalism could offer individuals only a share in general progress and so looked 'negative'; (p. 69) some used liberal phraseology to defend antisocial privileges; (p. 69) success made remaining evils seem both unbearable and unnecessary.
  **Conclusion**: (pp. 68-69) the success of liberalism caused its decline; the abandonment was a mistake of impatience, not a refutation.

- **Reversal, not continuation.**
  **Premises**: (p. 69) each step looked like a difference of degree; (p. 69) cumulatively the question has changed from how to use spontaneous forces to how to replace them by conscious direction; (p. 70) Mannheim says outright that mankind is tending to regulate the whole of its social life.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 69) the change is a complete reversal of the trend of modern European history.

- **German-provenance argument.**
  **Premises**: (p. 70) from about 1870 Germany became the centre from which ideas spread; (p. 70) Germany led in socialist theory and practice by a generation; (p. 71) German thinkers themselves set their ideas against 'Western' liberalism and democracy; (unstated) ideas carry their political consequences with them when transplanted.
  **Conclusion**: (pp. 70-71) the collectivist ideas now dominant in England and America are imports from the tradition that produced the totalitarian regimes.
