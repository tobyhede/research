What role has the progressive income tax (and its twin, the progressive estate tax) played in shaping twentieth-century inequality, why is it now under threat, and how high should the top marginal rate be? Piketty's answer: progressive taxation was born of the world wars more than of democracy, and its purposes were never thought through (p. 360), (p. 364); the United States and Britain invented "confiscatory" top rates, whose aim was to end very high incomes and fortunes rather than to raise revenue (p. 370); the collapse of those rates after 1980 explains the explosion of top executive pay through a bargaining mechanism, not a productivity one (pp. 373-376); and the optimal top rate on incomes above $500,000 or $1 million is "probably above 80 percent" (p. 376).
