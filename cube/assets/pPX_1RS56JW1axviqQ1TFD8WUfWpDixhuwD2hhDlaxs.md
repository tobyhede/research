| Term | First used | Author's meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Blind" competition | p. 130 | Competition is "no respecter of persons": rewards depend on capacity and luck, not on anyone's view of merit. Offered as a commendation, by analogy with blindfolded justice. |
| Impersonal forces / impersonal process | p. 131, p. 133 | Outcomes that are "nobody's conscious choice" (p. 133), where "no person's view about what is right and desirable overrules that of others" (p. 131). The contrast terms are "the deliberate decision of authority" (p. 133) and "design" (p. 134). |
| Chance / luck | p. 131 | Admitted to be "often as important as skill and foresight" in competition. Treated as acceptable because it is not a person's will. |
| Equality of opportunity | p. 131 | Chances are not equal under free enterprise; there is "a strong case" for reducing this inequality as far as congenital differences allow and without destroying the impersonal character of the process. |
| Freedom (of the poor) | p. 131 | Absence of "absolute impediments", of "dangers to bodily security and freedom", of being confined "by brute force" to an assigned task and place; having no one able to prevent one's attempt. Explicitly not equal range of options. |
| Private property | p. 132 | "the most important guaranty of freedom" (p. 132), for non-owners almost as much as owners, because it means control of the means of production is divided among many independent people. The value lies in dispersal, not in ownership as such. Free enterprise is "necessarily" based on it, and "though perhaps not with the same necessity" on inheritance (p. 131). |
| Power | p. 132 | Power over persons: ability to determine "the income and position of particular people". State ownership of all means does not transfer existing power; it creates a new power "which in a competitive society nobody possesses". A millionaire's power is "very much less" than that of the smallest official wielding coercion. |
| Planning | p. 132, p. 134 | State use of its ownership of the means of production "in full awareness of all these effects" on incomes (p. 132); "planning the whole economic life" (p. 134); "a totally planned system" (p. 135). Also "planning for the sake of justice" (p. 134). |
| Distributive justice | p. 135 | "Somebody's ideal" of who should have what; a "vague idea" given clear meaning only by absolute equality (p. 136). Requires answering "innumerable questions of relative merits" (p. 135). |
| Equality (absolute) | p. 136 | "Complete and absolute equality of all individuals in all those points which are subject to human control"; "mechanical equality". The only rule that would give the planner definite guidance. |
| "Greater equality" | p. 136 | "Merely negative", a dislike of the present state; "hardly more definite" than "common good". In effect: "take from the rich as much as we can". |
| Just price / fair wage | pp. 136-137 | Either the customary price or wage, or what would obtain without monopolistic exploitation. Standards derived from competition that would disappear with it. |
| Who, whom? | p. 135 | Attributed (tentatively) to Lenin: who plans, directs and assigns station to whom. The "universal problem of a socialist society". |
| Totalitarian (versus free) system | p. 135 | The difference lies in "the extent of the activities of the government": whether everything a person gets depends on government, or only "some people will get some things in some way at some time". |
| Weltanschauung / creed | p. 138 | A single common view of essential values that planning needs to justify a particular plan; accepted, not rationally demonstrated. |
| Privilege | p. 142 | Here: the advantages a movement "frankly promised" to the classes it appealed to under a new hierarchical order. Consistent with ch06 (advantage assigned by authority) but now used for whole classes. |
| Socialism (extended) | p. 140 | Any wish for "a deliberate sharing-out of wealth according to their ideas of justice" by state control of economic activity; in this sense the lower middle class and the fascist movements were socialist ("rival socialist factions", p. 140). |
| Labor aristocracy | p. 140 | The better-paid organised industrial workers whose relative gains provoked the "new underprivileged class". |
