**r > g is the main force for divergence.** When the return on capital exceeds growth, past wealth outgrows the economy. Piketty treats this as a historical fact, not a law of logic. - **Author's claim:** with a return on capital of about 4–5% against growth of 0.1–1%, a rentier needs to reinvest only a fifth of capital income for inherited wealth to keep pace with the economy. Saving more concentrates wealth further. This is why wealth was so concentrated before 1914. [Ch 10, line 10644](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L10644) [Ch 10, line 10651](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L10651)
- **Reasoning:** for most of history g was close to zero, while r was rarely below 2–3% before tax. Under random shocks to fortunes, the equilibrium wealth distribution has a Pareto tail whose inequality rises steeply with r − g. [Ch 10, line 10697](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L10697) [Ch 10, line 11031](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L11031)
- **Evidence:** French probate records from 1791 onward show no fall in concentration before 1914. Paris's top centile rose to over 70% of wealth by 1913. [Ch 10, line 10322](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L10322) [Ch 10, line 11080](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L11080) In the twentieth century, for the first time, the return net of taxes and losses fell below growth. [Ch 10, line 10776](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L10776)
- **Boundary:** Piketty concedes no evidence assigns precise weights to each mechanism. His world r and g series are “approximate and uncertain,” and the model linking r − g to wealth shares sits in the notes. [Ch 10, line 10638](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L10638) [Ch 10, line 10734](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L10734) **[Analysis]** The argument uses a single economy-wide r, although the same chapter shows rentiers consuming their capital and returns differing by fortune size.
- **Application [Application]:** when judging whether wealth will concentrate, compare the after-tax return the wealthy actually get with the growth rate of incomes, not the headline return.
