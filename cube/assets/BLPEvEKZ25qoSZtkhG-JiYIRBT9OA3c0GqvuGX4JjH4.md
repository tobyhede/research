- **Data quality caveats**: tax covers a small minority in poor countries, so only top-centile shares are estimable; South Africa's top decile reaches 50–55 percent where data allow (p. 237); breaks around independence (p. 237); deterioration after 1990 (pp. 237-238; notes 29-31). India's 2010 point rests on "very imperfect data" on managers' pay (note 31).
- **India's "black hole"**: rising top share explains a quarter to a third of the 1990–2000 gap between national-accounts and survey growth (p. 238); the gap is nearly half of measured growth, about 4 versus 2 percent a year per capita (note 32; Banerjee and Piketty 2005).
- **German records**: state income taxes from 1880–1890, no national records before World War I, frequent breaks in the 1920s, none for 1938–1950, data only every three years, changing borders (p. 235).
