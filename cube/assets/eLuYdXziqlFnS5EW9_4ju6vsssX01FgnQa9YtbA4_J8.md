- **"Logical contradiction" vs "historical fact"**: ch10 states r > g is "a historical fact, not a logical necessity" (p. 255) and "a historical reality dependent on a variety of mechanisms" (p. 261). The Conclusion's "fundamental logical contradiction" (p. 427) and "fundamental structural contradiction of capitalism" (p. 427) upgrade it. The Introduction and ch10 labelled it the fundamental force for divergence (pp. 22, 254). A cross-book synthesis should quote ch10's formulation as Piketty's considered view and treat the Conclusion's as rhetoric with a Marxian echo.
- **Certainty and policy confidence**: the Conclusion's epistemic modesty (p. 426) sits beside unqualified claims that r > g "will again become the norm" as "likely" (p. 427) and that the progressive capital tax is "The right solution" (p. 427).
- **Market imperfection**: the claim that r > g is not a market imperfection (p. 428) restates ch11 (p. 309). It is the core of Piketty's disagreement with readings in which better-functioning markets would equalise; it also means the case for the capital tax does not depend on market failure.
- **Note 1's concession** (p. 510): if a higher g raises r, the book's policy space narrows to taxation; this links to ch06's discussion of the elasticity of substitution and the response of r to β.
- **Growth ceiling**: "no matter what economic policies are adopted" (p. 427) is the strongest form of ch02's claim; ch16 notes that climate change may push the other way (p. 424).
- **Method vs practice**: the critique of "immoderate" mathematical modelling (p. 429) applies only partly to the book itself, which uses a small set of accounting identities and a steady-state law (β = s/g) heavily. The link is to the Introduction's statement that the book is "as much a work of history as of economics" (p. 28).
- **Scale of democracy**: the European-democracy wager (p. 428) is the end point of ch15 (global tax as a "utopia") and ch16 (budgetary parliament, pp. 416-417). The nation-state's remaining role (p. 428) echoes ch13's modernised social state.
- **Marikana and Furet**: the Conclusion closes the frame opened in the Introduction (Marikana, p. 31; the 1789 Declaration) and closed in ch16 (Lonmin accounts, p. 426): numbers and transparency as weapons for the least well-off.
- **Cross-book**: the claim that scholars cannot confine themselves to means while citizens choose ends (p. 429) is a direct rejection of the value-free, means-only economics that a Weberian or Hayekian position would defend. Useful for a themes synthesis on economics as science vs political economy.
