**The remedy is a progressive global tax on capital, plus transparency.** An annual tax on net wealth, backed by automatic exchange of banking information. Its purpose is to regulate capitalism more than to raise revenue. Top income-tax rates above 80% and a one-off capital levy on public debt complete the package. - **Author's claim:** Piketty proposes a progressive annual tax on individual net wealth, covering all assets with no exceptions. An illustrative schedule is 0% below €1 million, 1% between €1 and 5 million, and 2% above €5 million. [Ch 15, line 15599](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L15599) [Ch 15, line 15607](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L15607) Its main justification is ability to pay: for the very rich, declared income can be a tiny fraction of economic income. [Ch 15, line 15870](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L15870)
- **Reasoning:** even a low rate would force reporting and so create transparency about wealth, which makes financial regulation possible. That requires automatic exchange of banking information, enforced by sanctions on countries that refuse. [Ch 15, line 15654](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L15654) [Ch 15, line 15751](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L15751) Rates of about 5% on the largest fortunes would be needed just to stop further divergence. [Ch 15, line 16028](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L16028)
- **Companions:** top income-tax rates “probably above 80 percent” on incomes over $500,000–1 million, aimed at curbing top pay rather than raising revenue. [Ch 14, line 15474](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L15474) For public debt, a one-off progressive capital levy is better than inflation, and austerity is worst. [Ch 16, line 16400](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L16400) The euro needs a Eurozone budgetary parliament. [Ch 16, line 17003](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L17003)
- **Boundary:** Piketty calls the tax “a utopian idea” that needs unrealistic cooperation, and says Europe is the place to start. [Ch 15, line 15555](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L15555) [Ch 15, line 15561](https://github.com/tobyhede/research/blob/main/books/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century/source/source.txt#L15561) **[Analysis]** The revenue estimate (about 2% of European GDP) assumes market valuation of unlisted firms and does not quantify avoidance.
- **Application [Application]:** judge any wealth-tax proposal first on whether it has a financial cadaster, meaning complete and automatic reporting of who owns what.
