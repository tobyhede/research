- **Argument from the twice-watched trend (the book's warrant).**
  **Premises**: (p. 57) the influences on thought in civilised nations are largely similar but operate at different times and speeds; (p. 57) the author heard in England and America opinions and proposals he had met twenty or twenty-five years earlier in the German-speaking world; (p. 58) he spent half his adult life in Austria and half in the US and England; (unstated) similar opinions, once dominant, tend to produce similar institutions and political outcomes regardless of differing national traditions; (unstated) the German outcome was caused by those opinions and not mainly by defeat, inflation, depression or the Versailles settlement.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 57) the recurrence suggests, 'if not the necessity, at least the probability', that developments will take a similar course; Germany's fate is the one the democracies risk repeating.

- **Argument that naziism is an outcome of socialism, not a reaction to it.**
  **Premises**: (p. 58) the internal regimes of communist Russia and National Socialist Germany share many repellent features; (p. 61) socialists were the only numerically significant opposition to the Nazis, which shows liberalism in the old sense had already been driven out; (p. 61) the Nazi quarrel with the Left is a quarrel of rival socialist factions; (p. 62) what Germany, Italy and Russia had in common was socialist opinion, not Prussianism; (unstated) a feature shared by all cases of an outcome and absent elsewhere is its cause.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 58) the rise of fascism and naziism was 'a necessary outcome' of the preceding socialist tendencies. The demonstration is deferred: 'As we hope to show' (p. 61).

- **Argument against the national-character explanation.**
  **Premises**: (p. 60) Mill's *On Liberty* drew on Goethe and Humboldt, while Carlyle and H. S. Chamberlain, forebears of National Socialism, were British; (p. 60) the national-character view borrows the worst of German racial theory; (p. 61) it works as escapism, sparing us the re-examination of beliefs taken over from the Germans.
  **Conclusion**: (pp. 60-61) the thing to explain is the victory of a set of ideas under particular circumstances, not German viciousness.

- **Argument from non-inevitability to the point of the book.**
  **Premises**: (p. 59) the movement toward socialism is driven by what nearly everybody wants, not by objective facts; (p. 59) those whose convictions drive it might recoil if they saw where it leads; (p. 58) if the development were inevitable there would be no point in writing.
  **Conclusion**: (p. 58) the development can be prevented if people realise in time where their efforts may lead. Ideas are the operative cause, so persuasion is the remedy.

- **Wartime-relevance argument.**
  **Premises**: (pp. 59-60) the democracies showed insecurity and uncertainty of aim toward the dictators, explicable only by confusion about their own ideals; (p. 60) features of Hitler's system have been recommended for imitation by people unaware they are integral to it.
  **Conclusion**: (pp. 59-60) understanding the forces behind National Socialism is needed to understand the enemy, counter propaganda and build the post-war world.
