- Is the dichotomy "will of a few persons" versus "impersonal process" exhaustive (p. 131)? Tax-and-transfer systems change the distribution by general rule without anyone assigning individual shares. Does anything in chs. 6-9 rule them out, or does his own p. 131 concession admit them?
- Which measures does he have in mind for reducing inequality of opportunity (p. 131) and for preventing mass unemployment (p. 134)? Collect with ch09 and with the inheritance aside (p. 131), which leaves room for limits on inheritance.
- Does the claim that moral standards of fairness "would necessarily disappear" with competition (p. 137) rest on anything beyond the two definitions of "just price"? Wartime rationing and wage norms are obvious test cases.
- The class account of fascism (pp. 139-141): how well does it fit the evidence on Nazi support, and does it treat inflation and depression as products of "socialist policy"? Compare ch12 and the editor's introduction.
- "no avenue to wealth and honor would exist save through the government" (p. 134): does the divided-property argument presuppose competitive labour markets? What does Hayek say about private monopoly and company towns (ch04, ch13)?
- If private property protects non-owners only because owners are many and independent (p. 132), does concentration of ownership weaken the argument by his own standard? Test against his treatment of monopoly.
- The comparison of the English unskilled worker with the Russian engineer (p. 131) mixes the effects of planning with the effects of dictatorship. Can the book separate them, given ch05's claim that the first leads to the second?
- Quotation accuracy: Disraeli (p. 134) is inexact and several citations in this chapter were wrong in the original (pp. 142-143). Later use of these quotations should cite the editor's corrections.
