| Term | First used | Author's meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Propaganda | p. 168 | Not objectionable or distinctive in itself; neither propaganda nor its techniques are peculiar to totalitarianism. What changes its nature is that all of it serves one goal under a single control, making its effect different in kind, not just magnitude, from propaganda by independent competing agencies (p. 168). At p. 176 used neutrally: new ideas are put to the test of argument and propaganda. |
| Gleichschaltung | p. 168 | The coordination of all minds in one direction (p. 168). Editor: usually translated coordination; Nazi coordination of all activities in support of the state (pp. 176-177, note 2). |
| Totalitarian propaganda (object and scope) | p. 169 | Propaganda that cannot confine itself to values but must extend to questions of fact (p. 169). |
| Myth / official doctrine | p. 170 | A theory, i.e. assertions about connections between facts, constructed (not necessarily consciously) to rationalize the planner's likes and dislikes, which becomes part of the governing creed and must not be questioned (p. 170). Examples: racial doctrine, Blut und Boden, Plato's noble lies, Sorel's myths, Mussolini's corporative state (pp. 170-171). |
| Perversion of language | p. 171 | Keeping old words while changing their meaning so that allegiance transfers from old values to new; a continuous process that ends with words as empty shells used only for emotional association (pp. 171-172). |
| Liberty / freedom (the perverted sense) | p. 171 | "Collective freedom", "planned freedom": not the freedom of members of society but the unlimited freedom of the planner; the confusion of freedom with power carried to the extreme (p. 171). Hayek's own sense is left as "liberty as we understand it" (p. 171). |
| Truth | p. 174 | Old meaning: something to be found, with the individual conscience the sole arbiter of whether evidence (or the standing of those proclaiming it) warrants belief. Totalitarian meaning: something laid down by authority, believed in the interest of unity, alterable as the organized effort requires (pp. 174-175). |
| Intellectual freedom / freedom of thought | p. 175 | Its value is not that everyone thinks independently but that any cause or idea may be argued by somebody; the prime mover of intellectual progress (p. 176). |
| Growth of reason | p. 176 | A social, interpersonal process arising from the interaction of individuals with different knowledge and views; unpredictable in its results, hence unplannable (p. 176). |
| Individualism | p. 176 | Here an epistemic attitude: humility before the social process and tolerance of other opinions; opposite of the intellectual hubris behind comprehensive direction (p. 176). |
| "Conscious" control | p. 176 | The collectivist demand; said to confuse individual reason, which alone can consciously control anything, with the interpersonal process by which reason grows (p. 176). |
