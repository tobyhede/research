The Conclusion introduces almost no new evidence; it refers back to the book.

- **Growth and return figures**: frontier growth 1–1.5%, return 4–5%, catching-up economies (postwar Europe, China) able to grow at 4–5% (p. 427). These restate ch02 and ch10.
- **Historical claim**: r > g held "throughout history until the eve of World War I", and two world wars reduced the return (p. 427), restating chs. 3–5 and 10.
- **Tax schedule**: the illustrative rates (p. 427) are ch15's (pp. 380, 390).
- **Normative source**: Article 1 of the 1789 Declaration of the Rights of Man and the Citizen, "common utility" (p. 428), returning to the book's epigraph.
- **Historiography**: Jean Bouvier, François Furet and Marcel Gillet, *Le mouvement du profit en France au 19e siècle* (1965), opening sentence quoted (p. 430); Furet and Jacques Ozouf, *Lire et écrire* (1977) (p. 430); Furet's work on the French Revolution (p. 430). Conclusion note 2: Sartre, Althusser and Badiou treat capital and class inequality as "of only moderate interest" (p. 510).
- **Conclusion note 1** (p. 510): an increase in g could raise r and so not reduce r − g (pointing back to ch10). This is a significant qualification of the claim that growth-promoting policy cannot close the gap: it suggests that even if growth could be raised, it might not help.
- **Methodological claims** about mathematical models and controlled experiments (p. 429) are unsupported by examples; no model or experiment is named.
