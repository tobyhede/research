What new institution could let democracy regain control over globalized patrimonial capitalism, how would it work, and is there any alternative? Piketty's answer: a progressive annual tax on individual net wealth, applied worldwide (or at first regionally, in Europe), backed by automatic international exchange of banking information (pp. 378-380). Its primary purpose is not revenue but to regulate capitalism: to halt the indefinite rise of wealth inequality and to make finance transparent (p. 381). Alternatives (protectionism, capital controls on the Chinese model, redistribution of oil rents, immigration) are inferior or only partial (pp. 395-399). The tax is a "useful utopia" (p. 378): unrealistic globally, but a standard against which other proposals are measured.
