| essentially critical task | p. 234 | Hayek's description of the book's job: to criticise, not to lay out a program. Repeated as the essentially critical character of the essay in the Bibliographical Note (p. 236). |
| detailed blueprint | p. 235 | A full design for a desirable internal order of society; Hayek doubts its use and doubts anyone is competent to supply it. |
| principles | p. 235 | What agreement is needed on now, in place of a blueprint; left unspecified here beyond freedom for the individual (p. 236). |
| plan progress | p. 235 | Hayek's scare-quoted label for the planners' aim, set against creating conditions favourable to progress. |
| obscurantism | p. 235 | The view that what was done in the recent past was all either wise or inevitable. |
| New Order | p. 235 | The programme of those who project the tendencies of the last forty years forward and imitate Hitler. |
| policy of freedom for the individual | p. 236 | The guiding principle Hayek calls the only truly progressive policy. |
