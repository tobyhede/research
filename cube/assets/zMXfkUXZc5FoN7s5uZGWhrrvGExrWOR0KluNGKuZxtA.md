- **Mechanism and conceptual argument**: the main support for A-E (pp. 130-138).
- **Comparative assertion without data**: the English unskilled worker versus the German small entrepreneur and the Russian engineer (p. 131). Supported only by a list of freedoms (changing job, residence, views, leisure).
- **Rhetorical questions in place of evidence**: "Who can seriously doubt", "who will deny" on the minority member, the millionaire versus the fonctionnaire, and the wealthy-powerful world (p. 132).
- **Testimony against interest**: Max Eastman, "as prominent an old communist", on private property and freedom, and on Marx's own view that private capitalism was a precondition of democratic freedoms (pp. 132-133).
- **Data, second-hand**: Hayek's note 2: Eastman, from official Russian sources, on a highest-to-lowest salary ratio of about 50 to 1 in Russia, as in the United States; Trotsky via Burnham, that the upper 11 or 12 per cent of the Soviet population received about 50 per cent of national income against 35 per cent for the top 10 per cent in the United States (p. 142). Hayek hedges: "What little information we have". Used to show that abolishing property income need not reduce inequality.
- **Quotation, authority**: Mill on a handful of human beings weighing everybody (p. 138); Disraeli's "no avenue to wealth and honor" (p. 134); Acton epigraph (p. 130).
- **Quotation from opponents or observers**: the "who, whom?" phrase, attributed with "I believe it was Lenin himself" (p. 135), sourced only to Muggeridge and Feiler (Hayek's note 5, p. 143); Lasswell's "who gets what, when, and how" (p. 135); the Austrian socialist Georg Wieser on organisations "for every field of activities" (p. 139); Hendrik de Man on anti-capitalist resentment turning against the socialist movement (Hayek's note 10, p. 143).
- **Historical example / sociological claim**: socialist parties as originators of youth organisations, party sport, greetings, cells; Balilla, Hitlerjugend, Dopolavoro, Kraft durch Freude as "little more than imitations" (p. 139). Supported by the one Wieser quotation and Hayek's own knowledge of Austria; English "book clubs" offered as a parallel (Hayek's note 9, p. 143).
- **Historical claim on the social base of fascism**: early Nazi rank and file poorer in money income than the average trade unionist; lower-middle-class envy as the largest single economic factor (p. 140). Introduced with "There can be little doubt" and "Nor can there be much doubt"; no figures or sources.
- **Illustration**: income spread among doctors, architects, plumbers, grocers "as great as those between the propertied and the propertyless classes" (pp. 137-138). Asserted, no data.
- **Language outrunning evidence**: "in every real sense" (p. 131); "Dissatisfaction of everybody with his lot will inevitably grow" (p. 134); "must indeed inevitably become the central political problem" (p. 134); "would necessarily disappear soon after the disappearance of competition" (p. 137); "No socialist movement which aimed at complete equality has ever gained substantial support" (p. 136); "It was not the Fascists but the socialists" repeated three times (p. 139).
